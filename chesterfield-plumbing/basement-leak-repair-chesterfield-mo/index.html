    Frequently Asked Questions About Basement Leak Repair in Chesterfield

    Price depends on the source. Fixing a leaking fitting or supply line is usually a few hundred dollars. Crack injection and sump work cost more, and perimeter drains or exterior waterproofing can run several thousand. We quote after testing.

    Zoeller, Liberty and Wayne cover most of our installs because they are durable and parts are easy to get. Primary pumps get cast iron housings. For homes that lose power in storms, we add a battery backup pump or a water-powered backup where city water pressure is strong enough, and we put a high-water alarm on the pit either way.

    For sump pumps we mostly install Zoeller and Liberty, with Wayne for some battery backups. Cast iron housings outlast plastic ones. For crack injection we use two-part polyurethane or epoxy kits from established commercial suppliers, and for interior drains we use perforated pipe with a dimpled wall membrane from the same manufacturer so the pieces fit together.

    Most supply and drain repairs are finished in a few hours. A new sump takes most of a day. Stack and slab repairs run one to two days because surfaces get opened and patched.

    Crack repairs use two-part polyurethane or epoxy injection resin, depending on whether the crack is actively wet. Wet cracks get polyurethane. Our interior drains use rigid perforated pipe, which keeps its slope and can be jetted later.

    Most plumbing leak repairs in a basement are done in one visit. With wall access, plan on two to four hours. Seepage repairs vary more, from half a day for an injected crack to most of a week for a full interior perimeter drain, which includes cutting and removing the slab edge, setting pipe and stone, and pouring new concrete.

    Tightening a loose fitting or replacing a washer hose is a reasonable DIY task. Cracked drain stacks are not. Slab leaks and sump wiring need a pro, since a mistake can flood the floor or create a shock risk.

    Gutters and downspouts do most of the work. Keep them clear and extended well away from the foundation. Test the sump each spring and swap rubber washer hoses for braided ones every five years.
