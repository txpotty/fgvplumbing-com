    Grease Trap Sizing and Placement in Chesterfield, MO

    Gravity Grease Interceptor Installation in Chesterfield, MO

    Frequently Asked Questions About Grease Trap Installation in Chesterfield

    Expect roughly $1,500 to $4,000 for a small indoor trap and $7,000 to $20,000 or more for an outdoor gravity interceptor. A floor-recessed trap costs more than one set on the floor. Location drives the rest. Lids under traffic cost more, and so does repaving.

    You get the manufacturer's warranty on the unit and a written labor warranty from us on the installation. Factory terms vary, so we hand you the registration card or file it for you. Keep your cleaning logs. Some manufacturers ask for proof of regular service before they honor a claim on a cracked body or a failed baffle.

    Plastic hydromechanical traps often last 20 years or more, while coated steel traps can rust out in 7 to 15 depending on how harsh the wastewater runs. Concrete tanks can last decades, though hydrogen sulfide gas slowly eats the concrete above the water line. Cleaning habits matter most. Rough cleaning and long gaps between pump-outs shorten the life of every type.

    We start with a fixture list and the drain rate of each fixture. Each sink's volume and drain time give a flow rate, and the dish machine's spec sheet gives its flow. That gives the GPM figure. Indoor units match the GPM, and outdoor tanks get sized by volume and retention.

    Half a day for most indoor units. We set it, connect it, add the flow control and test it with water. Gravity interceptors take two to five days on site, plus time for the inspector to see the tank before backfill and again at final. Permits add lead time.

    Watch the pump-out bills. Rising service frequency means the trap has been outgrown or the baffles have failed. Smell is another clue. Slow floor sinks, grease odor at the trap, a lid that no longer seals, or a failed FOG inspection all point the same way, and we can tell you which it is in one visit.

    An under-sink hydromechanical trap with good access is usually a half-day job, including the flow control and a leak test. Recessing one into the slab adds a day or two for saw-cutting and patching. Outdoor tanks are different. Plan on two to five days for a gravity interceptor once the permit is in hand, depending on depth, soil and how much paving we have to restore.

    For indoor traps we lean on Zurn, Watts, Schier and Endura, because their parts are easy to find and their units carry PDI or ASME certification. Schier's plastic bodies do not rust, which helps in hot, wet kitchens. Brand matters less than sizing. A well-known trap installed too small will still fail inspection, so we pick the size first and the brand second.
