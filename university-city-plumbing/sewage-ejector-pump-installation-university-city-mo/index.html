    Sump Pump vs. Sewage Ejector Pump Installation in University City

    Sump pumps and ejector pumps look alike in a catalog but do very different jobs. A sump pump moves groundwater from a perimeter drain to the yard, while an ejector moves sewage from toilets and sinks to the sewer, and the ejector basin has to be sealed and vented because of the gas. Never mix the two.

    Ejector Pump Alarms and Backup Options in University City

    A high water alarm belongs on every ejector system, since a pump failure can lead to a sewage backup quickly. Audible alarms on their own power or Wi-Fi alarms that text you give warning while the pit is still filling, not after waste reaches the floor. A working sump pump and a backwater valve are smart protection for any home with a finished basement. Backup power can help.

    Laundry and Wet Bar Ejector Pumps in University City, MO

    Below-grade laundry rooms and wet bars send gray water down that still needs to be lifted to the sewer. A compact gray water pump handles it, sized for the 15 or more gallons a washer drops at once, with a lint filter to keep fibers off the impeller. Lint kills small pumps.

    Duplex and Commercial Ejector Systems in University City, MO

    Commercial buildings, multi-unit properties and busy homes may need two-pump setups with an alternating control panel. The panel switches the lead pump each cycle so wear is shared, and if the lead pump fails or the flow is heavy, the second pump starts automatically and the panel trips an alarm so someone knows to call for service. Redundancy matters here.

    Cost-Effective Sewage Ejector Pump Upgrades in University City, MO

    Upgrading an old ejector can often keep the existing pit. If the basin is sound, we can swap in a better pump, replace the old swing check with a new one, add a full-port shutoff and install a high water alarm, which covers most of what a new system offers for a fraction of the cost. We tell you if the basin is done.

    Liberty Pumps Sewage Ejector Pump Installation in University City, MO

    Liberty builds single and dual pump packages that arrive with the basin, pump, lid and fittings matched. That speeds up new installs, and their wide-angle float switches are less likely to hang up on the basin wall than older tethered floats, which is one of the most common reasons a pump fails to start. Stuck floats are common.
