    Electric Water Heater Replacement in University City

    Electric water heaters in University City usually fail from a burned-out heating element, a bad thermostat or a tank that has finally rusted through at the bottom. Elements are cheap to swap. A leaking tank cannot, so if water is coming from the tank body itself, we replace the heater and match the voltage, wattage and breaker size to what your panel already supplies. Most swaps take a few hours.

    Attic Water Heater Replacement in University City

    Some older neighborhoods still have lead service lines, which are worth replacing when other plumbing work is planned. Attic water heaters take more work to replace than ones in a garage or closet. Getting a 50-gallon tank up a pull-down ladder or through a narrow scuttle takes two people and care, and code calls for a drain pan with a line to the outside or a safe drain, a service platform and a light near the unit. Two-person crew, always.

    Frequently Asked Questions About Water Heater Replacement in University City

    Most replacements run $1,200 to $3,500, depending on the type of unit. Gas or electric, tank size, venting changes, code upgrades like an expansion tank or drain pan, and whether the heater sits in a garage or an attic are what move a job from the low end to the high end. We price it after a visit.

    It depends on age and the problem. If the heater is over 10 years old, breaks down often or the tank itself is leaking, replacement is usually the better value, while a newer heater with a bad thermostat or element is usually worth repairing. You get both numbers.

    A tank stores and keeps hot a set amount of water, while a tankless unit heats water only as it flows. Tanks are cheaper and simpler to swap, and tankless saves space, avoids standby loss and lasts longer, but needs more gas and new venting. Both have a place.

    We install all the major brands, including Bradford White, Rheem, AO Smith, Rinnai, Navien and State. Size, fuel and warranty guide the pick.

    Start with household size and how they use hot water. We check baths, tub size and peak hours, then choose by first-hour rating, the amount of hot water a tank delivers in a busy hour. Gallons alone can mislead.

    Water around the base, rusty hot water, popping or rumbling as it heats, and running out of hot water faster than you used to. A tank past 10 years old with any of those signs is usually worth replacing before it fails, since a ruptured tank can put 40 or 50 gallons on the floor in minutes. Age is a big clue.

    Most tanks carry a 6 to 12 year warranty from the maker on the tank and parts. FGV Plumbing adds a workmanship warranty on the install.
