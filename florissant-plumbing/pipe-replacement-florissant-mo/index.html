    What Goes Into a Repipe in Florissant, MO

    Whole-Home Re-Piping in Florissant

    Pipe Replacement for Multi-Unit Buildings in Florissant

    Apartments and condos share risers. Shared lines mean shared problems. We plan riser replacement in phases, one stack at a time, keeping each unit informed. Organized work.

    Galvanized Pipe Removal in Florissant, MO

    We often leave old galvanized pipe in the wall. It is disconnected and capped, while new lines run beside it, which keeps drywall damage low. Only accessible pipe comes out.

    PEX Pipe Replacement in Florissant

    PEX bends around corners and comes in long rolls, so a typical repipe needs fewer fittings than copper would on the same layout. Disconnecting hoses, insulating exposed lines and letting faucets drip on bitter nights head off most freeze damage. PEX can stretch a little in a freeze instead of splitting. We use PEX-a with expansion fittings where possible, which give a full-bore connection and hold up well over time. Faster install.

    PEX Pipe Installation in Florissant

    PEX costs less than copper and goes in faster. Scale and rust do not affect it. Groundwater from limestone areas is especially mineral-heavy, which makes softeners and regular water heater flushing worthwhile. We use PEX-a with expansion fittings or PEX-b with crimp rings depending on the job, choosing a layout that fits the house. Fewer joints, fewer leaks.

    PEX Repiping in Florissant

    PEX repipes go faster than copper. Flexible lines snake through walls with fewer openings, and home-run systems from a manifold let you shut off individual fixtures without killing water to the house. Popular choice.

    Galvanized Pipe Replacement in Florissant, MO

    Galvanized steel was common before the 1960s. The zinc wears off and the steel rusts inside. Over decades the opening shrinks, pressure drops and the water turns brown after sitting, while threaded joints corrode through first. Replacing it is the only fix.

    Corroded Pipe Replacement in Florissant, MO

    Corrosion shows up as green or white crust on copper, rust on steel, or pinholes that drip. Some water is harder on pipe. We replace corroded sections or the whole system depending on how widespread the damage is, including fittings near the heater. Look at the joints.
