    Clay and Orangeburg Sewer Replacement in Cape Girardeau, MO

    Orangeburg is tar-soaked wood fiber. It was installed in a lot of homes in the decades after the war, and over time it softens, blisters and squashes into an oval that catches everything going by. Clay holds up better, except at the joints. When the camera shows trouble in three or more places, a full replacement is usually cheaper than chasing spot repairs.

    Open-Trench Sewer Replacement in Cape Girardeau

    A collapsed run or a long belly usually means we dig. We open a trench along the old line, pull the broken pipe and lay new PVC on compacted bedding at a steady fall of an eighth to a quarter inch per foot. Sod gets cut and rolled aside first when the lawn allows it. The inspector sees open pipe. Backfill goes in compacted lifts so the trench line does not sink into a ditch a year later.

    Residential Sewer Replacement in Cape Girardeau, MO

    Most of our sewer work is single-family homes. Basements are common in many areas, so sump pumps and foundation drainage are an important part of keeping homes dry.     Cured-in-Place Sewer Lining in Cape Girardeau

    Lining builds a new pipe inside the old one. We clean the line first, often with a cutter and a jetter, then pull a resin-saturated felt tube into place and inflate it so it cures hard against the host pipe. It needs a pipe that still holds its shape. It is a strong choice under slabs and finished rooms. We say plainly whether your line qualifies.

    Spot Sewer Repairs in Cape Girardeau, MO

    Sometimes one joint is the whole problem. With only one bad section, we excavate a single small pit and splice in new PVC using shielded couplings. Far cheaper than a full run. We only suggest it when the footage backs it up.
