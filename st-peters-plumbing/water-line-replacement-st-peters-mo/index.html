    Copper Service Line Installation in St. Peters, MO

    Copper is still the choice for some homeowners. Plenty of copper mains have lasted 60 years.     Polybutylene Water Line Replacement in St. Peters, MO

    Polybutylene is the gray or blue plastic pipe installed from roughly the late 1970s to the mid 1990s. Chlorine in the water breaks it down from the inside. The failures tend to be sudden splits rather than slow drips, and some insurers will not write a policy on a house that still has it. Worth replacing.

    PEX Water Line Installation in St. Peters, MO

    PEX bends around corners, so a typical service run needs no fittings between the meter and the house. Pipes in exterior walls, crawlspaces and unheated garages are the usual trouble spots when temperatures drop sharply. A frozen PEX line often survives where copper would crack. We still bury it below the frost depth the local code sets, and we keep it out of direct sun at the meter box, since UV light breaks it down. Short exposure only.

    Pressure Regulator and Shutoff Upgrades in St. Peters, MO

    Street pressure varies a lot from one neighborhood to the next. Plumbing code caps house pressure at 80 psi. If your new line comes in above that, we add a pressure reducing valve right after the main shutoff, set it around 55 to 65 psi, and swap the old gate valve for a full-port ball valve. A quarter turn and the water is off.

    Galvanized Main Line Replacement in St. Peters

    Galvanized steel was common in homes built before the 1960s. Inside, the pipe rusts once the zinc is gone. After a few decades the opening can be half its original size, which is why pressure drops and the water runs orange after a shutoff, and no amount of cleaning brings it back. It has to come out.
