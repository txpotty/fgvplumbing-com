    Frequently Asked Questions About Grease Trap Installation in St. Peters

    Installed prices for small indoor hydromechanical traps typically fall between $1,500 and $5,000. Large outdoor gravity interceptors often run $8,000 to $30,000 or more once excavation and site restoration are counted. Brand is a small part of it. The written estimate holds unless the dig turns up a buried surprise.

    Plastic hydromechanical traps often last 20 years or more, while coated steel traps can rust out in 7 to 15 depending on how harsh the wastewater runs. Concrete tanks can last decades, though hydrogen sulfide gas slowly eats the concrete above the water line. Cleaning habits matter most. A trap that is scraped with a metal tool every week or left full of grease for months ages much faster than one that gets regular service.

    Indoor hydromechanical traps and outdoor gravity interceptors cover nearly every job we do. Automatic grease removal units are a pricier option for kitchens that fry all day. Most cafes go hydromechanical. High-volume kitchens usually get an outdoor gravity tank, especially if the sewer authority asks for one.

    On a commercial kitchen, no. Nearly every jurisdiction requires a licensed plumber to pull the permit and do the work. A homeowner adding a small under-sink trap to a home kitchen may be allowed to do it, but even then the flow control and vent have to be right or the trap siphons and does nothing. Ask the building department first.

    Zurn and Watts make up a lot of our indoor installs, with Schier and Endura when a plastic body makes more sense. Outdoor interceptors are usually precast concrete, or polyethylene where a crane cannot reach. Any certified brand works. What we will not install is an uncertified import trap, because inspectors reject them and the parts disappear after a year.

    Watch the pump-out bills. A trap that needs service far more often than it used to is either too small or broken inside. Smell is another clue. Slow floor sinks, grease odor at the trap, a lid that no longer seals, or a failed FOG inspection all point the same way, and we can tell you which it is in one visit.

    Size comes from flow, and flow comes from your fixtures. We measure each connected fixture and calculate the gallons per minute they can send at once. Gravity tanks use volume instead. Outdoor tanks get sized by the sewer authority's formula, which we run for you.

    From first call to final inspection, most indoor jobs take one to two weeks, and most of that is waiting on the permit and the part. Our time on site is usually one day. Outdoor jobs run longer. Gravity interceptors can take three to six weeks end to end because of plan review and tank delivery.
