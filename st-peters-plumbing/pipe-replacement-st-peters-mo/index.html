    What Goes Into a Repipe in St. Peters, MO

    Whole-Home Repiping in St. Peters

    Repiping sounds bigger than it is. Most homes stay livable during the job. We work room by room, restore water most evenings, and keep dust down with plastic sheeting and drop cloths, then patch every opening before we leave. Clean finish.

    Slab Leak Pipe Repair and Replacement in St. Peters

    Water under a slab shows up as a warm spot on the floor, a sound of running water, or a meter that spins with everything off. We locate the leak first. Then you have a choice between breaking the slab to fix that spot or rerouting the line through walls or the attic, which skips the jackhammer. We explain both.

    Slab Leak Pipe Replacement in St. Peters

    Rerouting is often better than digging. Jackhammering floors is a last resort. We run new pipe through the attic or walls to bypass the leaking line under the concrete, and usually costs less than repairing in place. Fast too.

    Copper Pinhole Leak Replacement in St. Peters, MO

    Pinhole leaks start small. First a damp spot, then drywall damage. They usually mean the copper wall has thinned from corrosion, and when one shows up, more are often on the way along the same lines. Repipe or patch, we explain.

    Galvanized Pipe Removal in St. Peters, MO

    Removing every piece is rarely needed. It is disconnected and capped, while new lines run beside it, so we avoid tearing open walls just to pull out dead pipe. Only accessible pipe comes out.

    Water Line Replacement vs. Repair in St. Peters, MO

    A single bad spot can usually be fixed. Several leaks in different rooms, rusty water throughout, or pipe that crumbles when we cut it tells us the system is failing everywhere, and patching just moves the next leak down the line. We show you what we find.

    Polybutylene Pipe Replacement in St. Peters

    Polybutylene is gray or blue plastic installed from roughly the late 1970s to mid 1990s. It degrades from the inside. Failures tend to be sudden splits at fittings rather than slow drips, which can cause insurance headaches. Replace it.

    PEX Pipe Replacement in St. Peters

    PEX bends around corners and comes in long rolls, so a typical repipe needs fewer fittings than copper would on the same layout. Hard freezes happen every winter in Missouri, and the northern part of the state usually sees longer cold spells than the south. It often survives freezing where copper would crack. We use PEX-a with expansion fittings where possible, which give a full-bore connection and hold up well over time. Faster install.
