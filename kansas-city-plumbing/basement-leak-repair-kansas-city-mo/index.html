    Sump Pump Installation & Repair in Kansas City

    A sump pump lives in a pit cut below floor level and kicks on when a float rises. Most failures come from a stuck float switch, a burned-out motor, or a check valve that lets the discharge water fall back into the pit and short-cycles the pump. We test yours with a bucket. A battery backup is worth adding where power drops in storms.

    Urgent Basement Leak Service in Kansas City, MO

    Basement leaks rarely wait for business hours. Tanks tend to split at night when nobody is watching. The on-call truck carries couplings, valves and a wet vac, so the first visit usually stops the leak and restores water to the rest of the house.

    Wall Leak Solutions in Kansas City, MO

    Damp walls without a crack often point to water soaking through porous block or concrete. Tape foil to the wall for a full day, and if the room side is wet it is condensation, not a leak. Condensation needs a dehumidifier. Seepage needs drainage outside or a channel inside.

    New Sump Pump Installation in Kansas City

    Installing a new sump means breaking out a section of slab, setting a lined pit, and running the discharge out through the rim joist. We use a cast iron pump in most homes and a 1-1/2 inch discharge with a check valve. Discharge cannot go into the sewer in most places. It usually ends outside, well away from the foundation, at a spot your local rules allow.

    Water Damage Assessment in Kansas City, MO

    Before any repair, we figure out where the water came from and how far it spread.     Sump Pump Repair and Installation in Kansas City, MO

    Plenty of basements rely on a sump pit to handle runoff and groundwater. A working sump pump and a backwater valve are worthwhile protection for any home with a finished basement.     Crack Injection for Basements in Kansas City

    Injection works on poured concrete walls, not block. We set ports along the crack, seal the face with epoxy paste, and pump in polyurethane or epoxy from the bottom port up until resin shows at the next port. Epoxy adds strength. For a crack that is actively wet, polyurethane works better since it reacts with water.
