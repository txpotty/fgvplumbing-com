    How We Keep Water Out of Walls in Springfield, MO

    Below-Grade and Basement Wall Waterproofing in Springfield

    Mid-century homes typically have copper supply lines, while newer houses rely on PEX and plastic drain pipe. Any wall below grade sits in soil that gets saturated after heavy rain or snowmelt, and that water pushes against the wall with real hydrostatic pressure. We inject cracks, coat the wall inside or out, and add drain tile wherever water collects at the footing. A sump goes in if groundwater sits high. Some basements never need a sump.

    Shower Wall Waterproofing in Springfield

    Tile and grout are not waterproof. Water passes through grout every time you shower, so the layer behind the tile, a membrane over cement board or a foam board system, is what actually keeps the studs dry. We tear out failed tile, install new backer, and apply Schluter Kerdi, RedGard or Hydro Ban at the thickness the manufacturer calls for. We reinforce corners, niches and valve penetrations with fabric or preformed pieces.

    Exterior Foundation Waterproofing in Springfield

    Exterior waterproofing means digging down to the footing and coating the outside of the wall. It is the most reliable fix for a leaking basement, because the water never touches the block or concrete. We apply a rubberized asphalt or polymer membrane, add a dimple board to protect it, and set perforated drain pipe in gravel along the footing so water has somewhere to go. Basements are common in many areas, so sump pumps and drain tile are an important part of keeping homes dry. Backfill goes in last.

    Crawl Space Vapor Barriers in Springfield

    Bare dirt in a crawl space releases gallons of water vapor a day into the air under your floor. A 10 mil or heavier polyethylene barrier, overlapped a foot at every seam and taped, cuts that off and keeps the joists and subfloor dry. We run it up the foundation walls and fasten it with termination bar. It is a cheap fix that makes a big difference.

    Commercial Walls Waterproofing in Springfield

    Commercial basements, elevator pits and mechanical rooms in Springfield take on water the same way houses do, just with more at stake. One wet electrical room can close a business for days. We schedule around operating hours, work with property managers on access, and follow the waterproofing specs written into the building plans for below-grade walls. Downtime stays short.

    Plumbing Leak Behind Walls in Springfield

    A drip from a shower valve cartridge, a sweated copper stub or a loose drain slip nut can soak a wall cavity for months before paint bubbles. We find it with a moisture meter, a thermal camera that shows the cool wet spot, and a pressure test on the supply lines. Then we cut a clean access hole. We replace the leaking fitting or pipe section. We pull wet insulation and run fans until the cavity reads dry.

    Shower Pan Leak Repair and Waterproofing in Springfield, MO

    Wet baseboards outside the shower or a stain on the ceiling below usually point to the pan. We plug the drain, fill the pan with an inch or two of water, and mark the waterline and check it a day later. A drop means a leak. Rebuilding means a mortar pre-slope of a quarter inch per foot, a new PVC liner or bonded membrane, and a clamping drain tightened onto the liner. We flood test the new pan before any tile goes down.

    Foundation Crack Repair & Sealing in Springfield

    Hairline cracks in poured concrete walls leak more water than their size suggests. We inject them with low-pressure polyurethane, which expands to fill the crack from the inside face to the soil side, or with epoxy when the crack also needs structural bonding. Horizontal cracks are different. A horizontal crack often signals soil pressure pushing the wall in, so we check for movement with a level before we seal anything. Most vertical cracks get sealed in one visit.
