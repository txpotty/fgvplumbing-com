    How We Install Showers in Springfield, MO

    Prefab Shower Unit Installation in Springfield, MO

    Prefab units are the fastest route to a new shower. The base goes on mortar, the drain and valve get connected and the walls are fastened so they stay rigid. They suit guest baths well. Usually a one-day job.

    Shower Valve and Fixture Installation in Springfield, MO

    Pressure-balancing valves are the baseline for any new shower. Thermostatic valves step up from there, holding an exact temperature and letting you set volume separately, which suits rain heads, handhelds and body sprays. Both prevent scalding. We help you choose based on how many outlets you plan.

    Digital Shower Systems in Springfield, MO

    Digital shower systems like U by Moen and Kohler DTV need a little more planning. The digital valve mounts in an accessible spot, often in a vanity or a closet, and needs low-voltage wiring to the controller plus a dedicated outlet near the valve. The outlet is an electrician's job. We plumb the valve and outlets.

    Curbless Shower Installation in Springfield

    Curbless showers need the floor lowered in the shower area. On a wood floor, we drop the joists or use a recessed pan system, and on a slab, we cut and lower the concrete so the finished floor runs flat into the shower. Linear drains help a lot. We plan the slope with your contractor before demo.

    Tiled Walk-In Shower Installation in Springfield

    Frameless glass walk-ins are popular, and the plumbing behind them has to be dead on. We install the valve, the head, an optional handheld and the drain, then set the mortar bed or foam pan to slope toward the drain at a quarter inch per foot. Then we test. We pressure-test the valve and flood the pan for a day before tile.

    Walk-In Shower Installation in Springfield

    Walk-in showers depend on floor slope. With no curb, the floor needs a steady quarter-inch-per-foot pitch to the drain and waterproofing that extends past the opening. The slope is our job. Grab bar blocking goes in while the walls are open, so bars can be added later without hunting for studs.

    Steam Shower Plumbing in Springfield

    Steam showers need four things: a generator, a water supply, a drain line and an electrical circuit. The generator goes within the maker's distance limit, and we plumb its supply, drain and steam line to the head. The circuit is an electrician's job. A sealed enclosure with a sloped ceiling keeps drips off you.

    Gym and Locker Room Showers in Springfield

    Gym showers run back to back from open to close. Commercial valves with metered or push-button controls keep run times in check, and the drains are sized for heavy use. Accessible stalls get handhelds on slide bars. We phase the work to keep some showers running.
