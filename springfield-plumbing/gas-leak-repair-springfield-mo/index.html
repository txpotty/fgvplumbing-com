    Propane Gas Leak Repair in Springfield, MO

    Propane behaves differently from natural gas because it is heavier than air, so a leak settles into basements and crawlspaces instead of rising. The first-stage regulator at the tank and the second-stage regulator at the house both get checked. Low spots get sniffed first. Your propane supplier owns the tank in most cases, so we coordinate with them for anything on the tank side of the first regulator. Rural properties with buried tanks get a line test too.

    Commercial Gas Leak Repair in Springfield

    Commercial gas piping often runs at 2 psi or higher with regulators at each appliance bank, so a leak test there is not the same as a house test. Regulator vents and lockup pressure get checked along with every joint. Rooftop units leak too. Where downtime costs you money, we isolate only the affected branch and keep the rest of the building running. Staff get a short walkthrough on the shutoff locations.

    Appliance Gas Line Repair in Springfield, MO

    Most appliance leaks show up at the flex connector or the shutoff valve behind the appliance, usually right at a flare nut where the two meet. Every time somebody slides the stove out to mop, the connector takes another bend. Valves weep at the stem. The fix is usually a new coated connector and a new ball valve, plus a drip leg if the appliance lacks one. Each joint gets a detector pass and a bubble test before the pilot is relit.

    Gas Line Replacement and Upgrades in Springfield, MO

    Adding a gas range or a tankless heater can overload pipe that was sized years ago for just a furnace and a water heater. We add up the BTU load and measure the run length before choosing a pipe size. Undersized pipe starves burners. Old pipe with heavy rust or bad threads gets replaced instead of patched, usually with black iron or CSST depending on access. A big tankless unit may also need a larger meter from the utility.

    Red Tag Gas Repairs in Springfield

    A red tag on your meter or appliance means the utility found a leak or unsafe condition and will not restore gas until a licensed contractor fixes it. The tag usually says what they found, so we start there. Then we test everything. After the repair, we run the pressure test the utility or inspector requires and send them the paperwork. Relights are scheduled with the utility, usually within a day or two.

    Permitting and Code Compliance in Springfield, MO

    Most jurisdictions want a permit for new gas pipe or a moved line, though a straight connector swap often does not need one. We tell you up front which way your job falls. The fee goes on the estimate. The work follows Missouri adopted fuel gas code and any local amendments. The signed inspection card goes to you, and a future buyer's inspector will likely ask to see it.

    Outdoor Gas Line Repair in Springfield

    Grills, fire pits, pool heaters and patio heaters often sit at the end of a long buried line that nobody has looked at since it went in. More outdoor lines get hit by shovels and fence augers than fail from rust. Dead grass is a tell. The outdoor branch gets isolated and tested by itself, then the bad run is replaced with polyethylene and a tracer. Above-ground stub-ups get a fresh riser and a shutoff valve.
