    What Goes Into an Ejector System in Springfield, MO

    Liberty Pumps Installation in Springfield

    Liberty ejector pumps show up on a lot of our installs, with cast iron bodies and two-inch solids handling on most sewage models. Their packaged systems come with the basin, lid, pump and check valve matched together, which cuts down on field fitting and gives the inspector a clean, listed assembly to look at. A full-port valve gets added.

    Sewage Ejector Pump Basin Installation in Springfield, MO

    The basin is where the system lives, and a 30-gallon size is common for a single basement bathroom. Bigger basins let the pump run longer, fewer times a day, which is easier on the motor and switch, so we size up when the pit serves a laundry or several fixtures. Lids must be gas-tight.

    Upflush and Macerating Toilet Systems in Springfield

    Macerating systems like Saniflo let you add a toilet and sink without breaking the concrete floor. The unit sits behind the toilet, grinds the waste and pumps it through a small discharge line, often three-quarter inch or one inch, up to the nearest drain, which makes it a good fit for a small basement half bath. Venting still applies.

    Emergency Sewage Ejector Pump Replacement in Springfield

    An ejector failure usually announces itself with the alarm, followed by slow drains and a smell near the pit. Shut off water to the basement fixtures, keep everyone from flushing below grade, and call us, since most emergency pump replacements are done in a few hours once the plumber is on site. We carry common pumps.

    New Construction Sewage Ejector Pump Installation in Springfield, MO

    On a new build, the basin goes in before the slab is poured. That is the cheapest time, since there is no concrete to cut, and we stub the drains, the vent and the discharge to the plans so the rough-in inspection goes smoothly. It keeps the pour on schedule.

    Grinder Pump Installation in Springfield

    Grinder pumps chop waste into slurry before pumping it, which lets them push through small pipe over long distances. A grinder makes sense when the sewer is distant, uphill or pressurized, such as a pressure sewer where each home pumps into a shared main. Grinders run pricier. For a basement bath near the drain, a standard ejector usually works.

    Ejector Pump Replacement in Springfield

    Ejector pumps usually last 7 to 10 years, depending on how often they cycle and what gets flushed. Signs of trouble include a pump that runs constantly, a grinding or humming noise, an alarm that keeps tripping and sewage odor near the pit, and any of those means it is time to call before the basement floor tells you. The float usually goes first.

    Ejector Pump Alarms and Backup Options in Springfield

    A high water alarm should be on any ejector basin, since a pump failure can lead to a sewage backup quickly. We install audible alarms with their own power source, or Wi-Fi alarms that text your phone, so you hear about a stuck float while the basin is still filling and not after waste has reached the basement floor. A working sump pump and a backwater valve are worthwhile protection for any home with a finished basement. Backup power can help.
