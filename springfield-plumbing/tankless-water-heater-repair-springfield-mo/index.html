    Tankless Water Heater Descaling Service in Springfield

    Scale is calcium and magnesium carbonate that comes out of hard water as it heats up, and a tankless exchanger heats water fast through narrow passages. Groundwater from limestone areas is especially mineral-heavy, which makes softeners and regular water heater flushing worthwhile. We hook a small pump and hoses to the isolation valves and circulate descaler for 45 to 60 minutes. Then we flush with clean water. The inlet screen gets cleaned too.

    Tankless Flush and Scale Removal in Springfield

    Lukewarm water at high flow and a unit that runs loud are two early signs of scale. White vinegar will do in a pinch. Commercial descaler works faster.

    Condensing Tankless Repair in Springfield, MO

    Condensing tankless heaters pull extra heat out of the exhaust and make acidic condensate that has to drain somewhere. That condensate runs through a trap and, where code or the manual calls for it, a neutralizer filled with limestone or marble chips before it reaches the drain. Clogged traps shut the unit down. We clean the trap, refill the neutralizer and check the PVC vent for sag or the wrong slope.

    Noritz Tankless Water Heater Repair in Springfield, MO

    Noritz heaters hold up well, but their flow sensors and vent connections still wear out. A code 90 points to combustion trouble, often a dirty fan or a restricted intake. Our tech cleans the burner and fan, checks every vent joint, and compares the flow sensor reading to the spec sheet. Gaskets get replaced if they leak.
