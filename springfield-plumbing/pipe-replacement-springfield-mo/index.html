    PEX Pipe Installation in Springfield

    PEX costs less than copper and goes in faster. Scale and rust do not affect it. Groundwater from limestone areas is especially hard, which makes softeners and regular water heater flushing worthwhile. We use PEX-a with expansion fittings or PEX-b with crimp rings depending on the job, and we run it in home runs from a manifold or in trunk-and-branch layouts. Fewer joints, fewer leaks.

    Burst Pipe Repair and Replacement in Springfield, MO

    Hard freezes happen every winter in Missouri, and the northern part of the state usually sees longer cold spells than the south. Ice inside a pipe cracks it open. Exposed pipe in crawlspaces, garages and outside walls is most at risk, and when it bursts we replace the damaged section with PEX or copper and add insulation or reroute it away from the cold. Then we test it.

    Polybutylene Pipe Replacement in Springfield

    Polybutylene is gray or blue plastic installed from roughly the late 1970s to mid 1990s. Chlorine breaks it down over time. Failures tend to be sudden splits at fittings rather than slow drips, and some insurers refuse to cover homes that still have it. Replace it.

    Drain Pipe Replacement in Springfield, MO

    Drain lines fail too. Cast iron cracks and scales. We replace cracked cast iron, failing ABS or old galvanized drains with PVC, checking slope and venting so the new drains flow freely. Camera first.

    Copper Pipe Replacement for Older Homes in Springfield, MO

    Older homes sometimes have copper that is thin from decades of use. Small leaks appear in random spots. We replace it with new Type L copper or PEX, matching the layout or improving it where the old routing was awkward. Better flow.

    Rusty or brown water is the big one. Low pressure, frequent leaks and stained ceilings are others. Pipe that is galvanized, polybutylene or more than 50 years old, especially if leaks are showing up in more than one room, usually points to replacement. Inspect first.
