    Remodel and Addition PEX Rough-In in St. Louis

    Rough-in heights matter more than people think. If the shower valve sits two inches low or the vanity stubs miss the cabinet, the wall gets reopened after tile, and that costs time and money. We check heights with your contractor before drilling. The test happens before drywall goes up.

    Commercial PEX Pipe Solutions in St. Louis, MO

    Apartment buildings, small offices and churches in St. Louis, MO are good candidates for PEX as much as houses can. Each unit can get its own manifold and shutoff, so a leak in one apartment shuts down one apartment instead of the whole stack. Rated walls need firestopping. Where PEX passes through a fire-rated assembly, we use listed firestop sleeves or sealant so the rating holds.

    Full Home PEX Repiping in St. Louis

    Galvanized steel rusts from the inside out. After 40 or 50 years the bore is so choked with scale that a half-inch line delivers a trickle, and the water comes out tinted brown after it sits overnight. We replace every foot of it with PEX. You get real pressure back, and the discolored water goes away with the old pipe.

    PEX-A Expansion Fitting Systems in St. Louis, MO

    With PEX-A, the tech slips a ring over the pipe end and stretches both with a battery tool. The fitting goes in while the plastic is still expanded, and the material's memory pulls it back down around the barb, sealing harder over the first minute. In the cold, it takes longer to shrink back. Joints get time to set before we pressurize.

    PEX Repipe Cost Comparison in St. Louis, MO

    Price on a repipe depends on a short list: bathroom count, square footage, how many stories, and how easy the attic and walls are to work in. PEX usually beats copper on price because the pipe costs less and a crew finishes in two to four days, not a full week. Patching is sometimes quoted on its own line. Ask and we will price all three, PEX-A, PEX-B and copper, side by side.

    PEX-A, PEX-B, and PEX-C Systems in St. Louis, MO

    There are three grades of PEX, named for how the plastic is cross-linked at the factory. PEX-A bends the easiest and uses expansion rings, PEX-B is stiffer and uses crimp rings or cinch clamps, and PEX-C handles more like B. Every one is listed for potable water. Pipe, fittings and tools come from the same maker on our jobs, which keeps the warranty valid.

    Uponor PEX System Installation in St. Louis

    Uponor's ProPEX system is PEX-A joined with an expansion tool and a cold-expansion ring. We stretch the pipe and ring, seat the fitting, and within seconds the plastic shrinks back around the barb into a tight seal. There is no crimp gauge to check. The fittings have a wide bore, so flow loss through each joint stays low.

    PEX-A vs. PEX-B Installation in St. Louis

    Homeowners ask us which one is better, and the honest answer depends on the job. PEX-A makes tighter bends, can be heat-fixed if it kinks, and its fittings flow better, while PEX-B is cheaper and its crimps hold just as well when each one is gauged. Both last decades. In a cramped attic, we usually pick PEX-A.
