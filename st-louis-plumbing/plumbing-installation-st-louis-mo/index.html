    What We Install in St. Louis, MO

    Whole-House Water Softener Installation in St. Louis

    Water in much of Missouri is moderately hard to hard, so scale gradually builds in water heaters, faucets and appliances. Softener sizing comes down to hardness times daily gallons, which tells us how many grains the resin has to remove between regenerations. Undersize it and it regenerates all the time, burning through salt. We set the drain line with an air gap. Then we program the regeneration schedule to your water and run a test cycle.

    Sewage Ejector Pump Installation in St. Louis, MO

    A basement bathroom sits below the sewer line, so gravity cannot carry the waste out. The fix is a sealed basin with an ejector pump that lifts waste to the main. Pump and basin are sized to the fixtures, the basin is vented to code, and the discharge gets a check valve and a shutoff so the pump can come out for service. Brands we trust here are Liberty and Zoeller.

    PEX Repiping in St. Louis

    Copper repipes cost more and take longer, which is why most whole-house repipes today use PEX-A or PEX-B tubing with brass or poly fittings. It runs quieter than copper, with less water hammer banging in the walls. Freeze damage is less likely too. Pipes in exterior walls, crawlspaces and unheated garages are the usual trouble spots when temperatures drop sharply. Fittings are matched to the tubing manufacturer.

    Tankless Water Heater Installation in St. Louis, MO

    Navien and Rinnai tankless units hang on a wall and free up the floor space a tank used to take. Most gas units need upsized gas piping and a new vent run in stainless or PVC. Electric models need amperage. We size by gallons per minute and the temperature rise from your incoming water. Rapid swings from mild to freezing weather expand and contract pipes and fittings, especially in older homes. Every unit gets isolation valves so it can be flushed.

    Sump Pump Installation in St. Louis, MO

    A sump pump that fails during a storm usually failed months earlier: a stuck float, a seized impeller, a dead check valve or a cord unplugged for the shop vac. The pit goes to proper depth, the primary pump is sized for the inflow, and a battery backup is added on request. Where the water exits matters most. The discharge gets carried well away from the foundation so the same water is not pumped twice.

    Water Heater Replacement in St. Louis

    Most tank heaters give out between 8 and 12 years, sooner with heavy sediment. Our stock covers 40 and 50 gallon tanks and 75 gallon units for larger families, and each install includes new connectors, a code relief line and a vent check. An expansion tank check is part of every job. Most installs finish in one visit.

    Gas Line Installation for New Appliances in St. Louis, MO

    Water heaters, ranges, dryers, fireplaces and pool heaters all pull from the same gas service. We add up the BTU demand of everything on the line, measure the run, and size each branch so the furthest appliance still gets full pressure. New lines get pressure tested, always. Bigger loads sometimes need a meter upgrade, and we set that up with the gas utility.

    Natural Gas and Propane Lines in St. Louis, MO

    Natural gas and propane run at different pressures, and the pipe sizing changes with them. We size every run from the appliance BTU ratings and total pipe length, and use black iron or CSST depending on the path. Code requires CSST to be bonded. Without that bond, a lightning strike nearby can arc through the tubing and start a fire.

    A sump pump moves clear groundwater out of a pit in the basement or crawl space. An ejector deals with waste, solids included, and lifts it from below-grade fixtures up to the sewer main or septic tank. They are not interchangeable.
