    Ejector pumps tend to run 7 to 10 years, depending on how often they cycle and what gets flushed. Watch for constant running, a hum or grind, repeated alarms and odor at the pit, since any of them means trouble is close. The float usually goes first.

    Grinder Pump Installation in St. Louis

    Grinder pumps chop waste into slurry before pumping it, which lets them push through small pipe over long distances. They are the right choice when the sewer is far away, uphill or under pressure, like on a pressure sewer system where the pump feeds a shared force main in the street. Grinders run pricier.     Ejector Pump Installation Cost Comparison in St. Louis, MO

    Residential Sewage Ejector Pump Installation in St. Louis

    Residential ejector systems in St. Louis serve basement bathrooms, lower-level suites and any fixture that sits too low to drain by gravity to the sewer. Each system needs a sealed basin, a pump with a float, a check valve and a vent. The parts are simple. Most problems come from the install, not the hardware, so we take our time on the vent and the lid seal.

    Liberty Pumps Sewage Ejector Pump Installation in St. Louis, MO

    Liberty builds single and dual pump packages that arrive with the basin, pump, lid and fittings matched. Matched packages speed up the install, and Liberty's float design is less likely to snag on the basin wall, a common reason older pumps fail to start. Switch failures cause many calls.

    Sewage Ejector Pump Basin & Pit Installation in St. Louis

    Digging the pit starts with a saw cut through the floor, deep enough to set the basin flush with the finished floor.
