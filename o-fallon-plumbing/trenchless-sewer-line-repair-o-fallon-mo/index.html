    Perma-Liner and NuFlow Lining Systems in O'Fallon

    Perma-Liner and NuFlow both make lining systems we use, each with its own resin chemistry and installation equipment. Both are built to ASTM standards for cured-in-place pipe. The brand matters less than the prep, because a liner bonded to a dirty, scaly pipe wall will not hold the way the manufacturer rates it. So the line gets jetted or descaled to bare wall before any resin goes in.

    Pipe Bursting in O'Fallon, MO

    Two pits are standard for a burst, one at the house end near the foundation and one out near the street or the city tap. The new HDPE pipe is butt-fused into one length first. The winch or hydraulic puller then drags the bursting head and the new pipe through. A 60-foot pull often takes under an hour.

    Sewer Camera Inspection for Trenchless Repair in O'Fallon

    Every trenchless job starts with a push camera fed in from the cleanout or a pulled toilet. The monitor shows us the pipe wall in real time, along with the footage counter, and a sonde in the camera head lets us mark the exact spot on the ground above it. The locator also reads depth, which matters for pit planning. No guessing where to dig.

    Cast Iron Sewer Line Lining in O'Fallon, MO

    Cast iron rusts from the inside out, and the bottom of the pipe goes first. That is called channel rot. We descale the pipe with chain knockers or carbide cutters spinning on a flexible shaft, which brings the wall back to bare metal so a liner can bond to it. Under a slab, lining beats breaking up the floor in two rooms.

    Cured-In-Place Pipe Lining in O'Fallon, MO

    CIPP stands for cured-in-place pipe. We wet out a felt tube with epoxy resin, invert it into the cleaned line with air pressure, and hold it against the old pipe wall until the resin hardens. Cure time runs a few hours. The finished liner seals cracks and leaking joints in the original pipe.

    Sectional Point Repairs in O'Fallon

    A point repair is a short liner, often 3 to 5 feet long, set over one bad spot. It rides in on an inflatable packer, guided by the camera footage, and the packer inflates to press the resin-soaked sleeve against the pipe wall until it cures. One crack, one patch. Sound lines with one bad joint are the right candidates.
