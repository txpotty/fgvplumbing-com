    Frequently Asked Questions About Basement Sump Pump Installation in O'Fallon

    Replacing a pump in an existing pit usually runs $500 to $1,200 including the pump and check valve. A brand new pit through the slab falls between $1,500 and $3,500, and a battery backup adds roughly $600 to $1,500. Most people add the backup.

    Watch for constant running, rapid cycling or grinding noises. Rust stains and a float that sticks are warning signs too, as is water sitting above the switch level without the pump starting. Do not wait for the storm.

    Discharge belongs in the yard, a dry well, or an approved drainage area at least 10 feet from the foundation. Most local codes do not allow sump water in the sanitary sewer, and it can draw a fine.

    Liberty Pumps, Zoeller and Wayne are our regulars. Each offers cast iron housings and dependable switches, and all three have solid parts support through local supply houses, which matters more than a fancy feature list when something breaks. The pit size picks the model.

    A 1/3 or 1/2 horsepower pump handles most residential basements. The real answer depends on inflow and lift, so we time the refill with a stopwatch and measure from the pit floor to the discharge exit before picking a model. Oversizing causes short cycling.

    Quality cast iron pumps usually carry three to five years of factory coverage. Our labor has its own warranty on top of that, and we walk you through the exact terms for the model you pick before we install it.

    Swapping an identical plug-in pump is doable for a handy homeowner. New pits, new outlets and discharge routing are a different story, because a mistake there leads to a cracked slab, a shock hazard, or water pumped right back against the foundation in O'Fallon. Those parts call for a licensed plumber.

    Sudden downpours often overwhelm older sewer systems, which can push water back up through basement floor drains.
