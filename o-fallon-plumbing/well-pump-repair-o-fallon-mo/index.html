    Well Pressure Tank Repair in O'Fallon

    Bladder tanks hold a cushion of air that lets the pump rest between cycles. A failed bladder lets the tank waterlog, and the pump begins to short cycle. The bladder, the switch and the tank fittings all get checked. A properly sized tank can add years to pump life.

    Low Water Pressure Repair in O'Fallon

    Weak pressure on a well is not always the pump. A clogged sediment filter, a pressure switch set too low or a leak in the yard line can all cause it. Sometimes the pump is fine and the well is simply producing less water than the house is asking for during peak use. We test each one.

    Well Pump Electrical Troubleshooting in O'Fallon, MO

    Electrical faults cause a big share of well calls, from a tripped breaker to a burned contact in the pressure switch. After that, we look at the capacitor and relay in the control box. Our technicians test circuits with the power locked out. All wiring and splices meet Missouri electrical code before the pump restarts.

    Jet Pump System Repair in O'Fallon, MO

    Jet pumps sit above ground and pull water up through a suction line with an ejector. Air leaks on the suction side cause most failures. FGV Plumbing checks the foot valve, the ejector nozzle and every suction fitting, then reprimes the pump and watches it reach cut-out pressure. A jet pump that runs nonstop without reaching pressure has almost always lost prime or sucked air through a cracked fitting.

    Grundfos Well Pump Repair in O'Fallon

    Grundfos SQ and SQE pumps have built-in electronics that protect the motor and report faults. Most Grundfos calls involve a sensor or a drive fault. FGV Plumbing reads the fault code, checks the wiring and tests the pressure sensor before replacing anything in a O'Fallon, MO well. Usually the electronics are fine.

    Pressure Switch Replacement in O'Fallon

    The pressure switch tells the pump when to start and stop. Most switches fail from pitted contacts, a plugged sensing tube or insects in the housing. We replace the switch, clean or replace the tube and set the cut-in and cut-out pressures to match your tank, typically 40 and 60 psi. One of the cheaper well repairs.

A submersible pull and replacement runs about $1,800 to $5,000 and up, based on depth and horsepower. Replacing an aging pump during a pull saves paying for a second pull later.
