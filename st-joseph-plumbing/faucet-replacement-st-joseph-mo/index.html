    Outdoor Spigot Replacement in St. Joseph, MO

    Outdoor spigots wear out from sun, hoses and hard closing. Pipes in exterior walls, crawlspaces and unheated garages are the usual trouble spots when temperatures drop sharply. We replace worn spigots with frost-free hose bibs where needed and add vacuum breakers for backflow protection, since code requires one on every hose connection. The wall cavity gets a look too.     Frequently Asked Questions About Faucet Replacement in St. Joseph

    Labor for a faucet replacement usually runs $150 to $350. With a solid mid-range faucet included, most jobs total somewhere between $300 and $750, depending on the model and the condition of the stops. High-end and touchless models can top $800.

    We work on every major brand. Delta, Moen, Kohler, American Standard and Pfister make up most of the faucets we replace, and we know their mounting hardware and cartridges by sight. Off-brand faucets are fine too.

    Plenty of homeowners manage it. Seized mounting nuts and corroded shutoffs in a cramped cabinet are what usually make the job harder than people expect it to be. A plumber can finish quickly without damaging the sink.

    Most swaps take one to three hours. Drilling a new hole in stone, replacing corroded stubs or cutting out a fused faucet adds time, sometimes most of a morning. We stay out of your way.

    A basic swap is doable with the right tools. Fused nuts and corroded stops are where DIY swaps go wrong, often leaving a cracked sink or a wet cabinet floor. That is a common call for us.

    Drips that come back after a repair are the clearest sign. Visible corrosion on the body, handles that grind or stick, a base that wobbles on the deck and low flow that cleaning the aerator does not fix all point toward replacement. Some people just want a new style.

    Ceramic disc cartridges matter more than the brand name. Moen, Delta, Kohler and Grohe all use them on most models and tend to hold up well for many years in mineral-heavy water. Clean aerators help too.

    The old faucet leaves with us. Removal and disposal are part of the price, and brass faucets go to scrap recycling instead of going to the landfill. No cleanup on your end.
