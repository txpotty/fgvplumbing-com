    Cracked Bowl or Tank Replacement in St. Joseph

    Sometimes only the tank is cracked, and if the model is still in production, a matching tank costs less than a whole new toilet. We check the model number first. If the bowl is the cracked part, the whole toilet gets replaced, since the bowl carries the seal to the floor and cannot be patched.

    Rental and Multi-Unit Toilet Replacement in St. Joseph, MO

    Landlords with several units do better standardizing on one toilet model with common parts, because the next flapper or fill valve is then the same part in every unit. We can replace several toilets in one visit. Tenants keep a working bathroom. We coordinate entry directly with them, so you do not have to be there.

    Wall-Hung Toilet Replacement in St. Joseph

    Wall-hung toilets mount to a steel carrier inside the wall, so replacing one means checking the carrier bolts, the gasket on the outlet and the condition of the wall finish around it. Some carriers need new bolts. We match the new bowl to the existing carrier whenever possible, which avoids opening the wall.

    Comfort-Height Toilet Replacement in St. Joseph

    Going from a standard bowl to a comfort-height model raises the seat two or three inches, which matters a lot for seniors and anyone recovering from knee or hip surgery. The drain location stays the same. If there is room, a grab bar beside it helps too, and we can anchor that into a stud during the same visit.

    Toilet Flange Repair During Replacement in St. Joseph

    Pulling the old toilet is the only chance to see the flange, and a cracked plastic ring or a rusted cast iron flange will ruin a new install within a year. It gets checked on every replacement. A split ring gets a stainless repair ring, while a flange that sits below the finished floor gets a spacer or extender so the seal compresses properly.

    Choosing a Toilet Brand in St. Joseph, MO

    Kohler, American Standard, TOTO, Gerber and Mansfield all make solid toilets, and each line has a few models we steer people away from because of weak flushes or hard-to-find parts. We will tell you which ones. Rough-in and bowl shape decide what fits, and we check both before you buy.

    High-Efficiency Toilet (HET) Upgrades in St. Joseph, MO

    High-efficiency toilets carry a WaterSense label and a MaP score, which tells you how many grams of waste the toilet clears in one flush. Look for a score of at least 800. A high score means fewer double flushes and fewer clogs, and that is where the real water savings come from.

    Replacing Pressure-Assisted Toilets in St. Joseph, MO

    Pressure-assisted toilets use a sealed vessel in the tank that stores air under line pressure and pushes water through the bowl hard, which helps in houses with long, low-slope drain runs. They are loud. When we replace one, we pick a vessel brand with parts that are easy to get later, and we check that house pressure is in the range the unit needs.

    Frequently Asked Questions About Toilet Replacement in St. Joseph

    A standard toilet replacement usually runs $400 to $800 with the new toilet and labor included, depending mostly on the model you choose. Any flange or subfloor repair is extra. Those get their own price before any work, so the base number does not move.

    Rough-in is the distance from the finished wall behind the toilet to the center of the drain, which you can find by measuring to the closet bolts. Twelve inches is standard. Some older houses have a 10 or 14 inch rough-in instead, and a toilet made for the wrong one either sticks out or will not fit.

    Replacement wins when repair costs start to approach the price of a new toilet, or when the toilet is old enough to waste water every time it flushes. Cracks settle it. Weekly clogs and a base that keeps rocking point the same way.

    Manufacturers usually cover the china for years and the tank parts for a shorter period, often one to five years depending on the brand. We add our own labor guarantee. If the install leaks, we come back.

    Watch for cracks, weekly clogs, a base that rocks after it has been reset, and a tank that uses 3.5 gallons or more. Age matters too. A toilet more than 25 years old is usually a good candidate even if nothing is broken yet.

    About two hours for a standard swap. In that time we remove the old toilet, inspect the flange, set the new one and test it, with the old fixture loaded on the truck at the end. Flange repairs add time.
