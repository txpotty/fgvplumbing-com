    Copper Pinhole Leak Repair in Liberty

    Pinholes form when the inner wall of copper pipe erodes or pits. Hot lines get it worst. They often appear in thin Type M pipe and on hot water lines where velocity and temperature are higher, which we fix by replacing that length with heavier pipe. More pinholes often follow.

    Slab Leak Detection and Repair in Liberty

    Copper often runs under concrete slabs in Liberty homes. Ground movement pulls on it. Expansive clay soils are common in Missouri, and they swell and shrink with moisture, putting stress on foundations and buried lines. We use acoustic and thermal equipment to find the leak without breaking concrete to search, so you can choose between a targeted slab cut and abandoning the line for a new route. We price both.

    Burst Copper Pipe Repair in Liberty, MO

    Copper in an exterior wall or attic can split when the water inside freezes. Disconnecting hoses, insulating exposed lines and letting faucets drip on bitter nights head off most freeze damage. Shut the main first. The split often does not leak until the ice thaws, so a pipe that froze overnight may start spraying the next afternoon once the house warms up. We cut out the split and solder in new pipe. Then we add insulation.

    Commercial Copper Pipe Leak Repair in Liberty

    Commercial buildings often run larger diameter copper for domestic water. Shutdowns cost money. We repair copper supply lines in offices, restaurants and apartment buildings, using press tools on large lines to shorten the shutdown and skip the torch. We schedule around business hours.

    Thin-Wall Copper Pinhole Repair in Liberty

    Older copper, especially Type M, pinholes more than heavier pipe. There is less metal to lose. We locate the leak precisely and either solder in a new section or use a press fitting near framing, replacing the damaged length with Type L copper so the repair has a thicker wall than the pipe it replaced. We check the rest of the run.

    Copper Fitting and Valve Repair in Liberty

    Angle stops, shutoff valves and copper fittings under sinks and behind toilets can weep or seize. Minerals jam them. Hard water buildup inside a valve can keep it from closing when you need it most, and quarter-turn valves go in to replace the old multi-turn stops. We test each one.

    Frequently Asked Questions About Copper Pipe Leak Repair in Liberty

    Accessible copper leak repairs typically cost $250 to $650. Hidden pipe runs higher. Repairs inside finished walls or ceilings usually range from $500 to $1,500 before drywall patching, and slab leak repairs on copper lines often run $1,500 to $4,000 depending on the method. You get the price in writing first.

    If you have had repeated leaks, it is worth pricing. PEX does not pinhole. PEX resists the corrosion and scale that cause copper pinholes and goes in quickly with fewer wall openings, so a PEX repipe often beats years of patching. We price both options.

    Keep water pressure under about 80 psi. A regulator helps. Treating hard or aggressive water, insulating pipe in cold spots and limiting how long a recirculation pump runs all reduce the wear that leads to pinholes and failed joints. Know where your main shutoff is.

    Clamps and epoxy putty can stop a drip for a day. Temporary fixes fail. A proper repair means cutting out the thin copper and soldering or pressing in new pipe, and a DIY patch that lets go while you are at work can flood a room in the time it takes to drive home. Use it only until a plumber arrives.

    Wet spots on walls, ceilings or floors are the obvious sign. Pressure drops count too. The sound of running water when nothing is on, a bill that jumps, or blue-green crust on exposed fittings all point to copper that is leaking or about to. The meter confirms it.

    Wall thickness. K is thickest. Type K is used for underground service lines, Type L is the standard for interior water lines in many codes, while Type M is lighter and more prone to pinholes over time. Our repairs use Type L.

    We work with Type K, L and M copper from all standard manufacturers. All are code-listed. We repair whatever is already in your walls, and we tie new copper or PEX into it with fittings rated for the pipe size and the transition. We default to Type L.

    Keep pressure in range. Test it yearly. Soften hard water, limit recirculation pump run time and get any green crust on fittings looked at early, while homes with older copper should have it checked every few years. Small fixes now save drywall later.
