    Why Polybutylene Fails in Liberty

    Polybutylene reacts with oxidizers like chlorine that water utilities use to disinfect tap water. The inner wall of the pipe and fittings slowly turns brittle, and small cracks grow until the pipe splits. Homes around Liberty on private wells should test for hardness and bacteria before picking a filter. Hot water speeds it up. Most of the failures we see are on hot lines and at fittings close to the heater.

    PEX Pipe Installation in Liberty, MO

    PEX is the most common replacement for polybutylene because it is flexible, resists scale and corrosion and installs faster than copper. Labor runs lower with PEX. We keep PEX out of direct sunlight, away from water heater flues and recessed light housings and support it at the spacing the manufacturer calls for, which protects both the pipe and the warranty.

    Full Home Repiping with PEX in Liberty

    Most poly replacements we do use PEX-A, a flexible tubing that runs long distances with few fittings. For slab houses, new PEX-A runs through the attic and drops down interior walls, so none of it sits under the floor. PEX handles a freeze better than copper. We use expansion fittings that keep the full inside diameter open, so pressure at the far shower stays strong.

    Finding Polybutylene Leaks in Liberty

    A poly leak inside a wall or ceiling can run for days before you see a stain. Watch the water meter. With every fixture off, a spinning leak indicator on the meter means water is going somewhere, and we use acoustic listening gear and thermal imaging to find it without opening walls at random.
