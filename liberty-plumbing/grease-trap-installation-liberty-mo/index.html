    Residential Grease Interceptor Setup in Liberty, MO

    Homes rarely need a grease trap, though some do. Licensed home bakeries and houses on old clay laterals that keep clogging are the usual cases. A compact under-sink unit handles the load, and a homeowner can clean it in a few minutes. Check with your local health office first.

    Restaurant Grease Trap Compliance in Liberty, MO

    Most sewer authorities run a FOG program with an inspector who opens your trap and checks your pump-out manifests against the schedule they set. Fail that and you can face fines or a surcharge on your sewer bill. We install to pass. We add a sampling port if they require it and size to the authority's own formula.

    Automatic Grease Removal Units in Liberty

    An automatic grease removal unit heats the trapped grease and skims it into a container on a timer, so staff empty a bucket instead of scraping a tank. The purchase price is higher than a passive trap. They also need a dedicated electrical circuit, which we coordinate with your electrician before the unit arrives. Fry-heavy kitchens like them most.

    Replacement and Upgrade Services in Liberty, MO

    Steel traps from decades back rust through at the baffle seams, and by then the outlet is usually passing grease straight to the sewer. A camera down the outlet line tells us how much damage the old unit already did. Sometimes the line needs jetting too. Upgrading also gives you a chance to move from a 20 GPM trap to a 35 or 50 if the menu or seat count grew.

    New Restaurant Build-Out Grease Traps in Liberty

    On a new build-out, the grease trap gets decided before the slab is poured, not after. The drawings and the equipment list from your kitchen designer tell us what will drain where.     Commercial Grease Trap Installation in Liberty

    A commercial kitchen with a three-compartment sink, a pre-rinse station and two floor sinks can push 50 gallons a minute of greasy water at the dinner rush. Our sizing starts from your peak flow, the worst ten minutes of the night, rather than a daily average. Undersized traps are the usual failure.
