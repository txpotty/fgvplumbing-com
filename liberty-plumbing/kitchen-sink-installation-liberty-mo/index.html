    Drop-In Kitchen Sink Installation in Liberty

    Sitting on the counter by their rim, drop-in sinks are a natural fit for tile, laminate and butcher block counters. Usually the fastest upgrade. We check the cutout size, seal the rim and tighten the clips evenly, and in most rentals and older homes the job is done in a couple of hours.

    Apron-Front Sinks in Liberty, MO

    Apron sinks need a cabinet cut to fit the front panel, and the sink has to be in place before the counter is templated. We set the sink and frame early, so the fabricator measures from the actual bowl instead of a spec sheet. That avoids an uneven reveal. Cast iron versions can top 150 pounds, so we bring two people.

    Farmhouse Sink Installation in Liberty, MO

    Apron-front sinks rest on a support frame built into the cabinet, not on the countertop. We build that frame from 2x4s or a steel bracket, set the sink dead level and leave the right reveal for the stone shop. Drain placement is planned early. Many farmhouse sinks drain at the back, and the trap has to work around that.

    Disposal and Dishwasher Hookup with Sink Installation in Liberty, MO

    Most new kitchen sinks get paired with a disposal and a dishwasher. We mount the disposal, run the dishwasher drain through an air gap or high loop and install a dedicated hot supply with its own shutoff. One visit costs less than three. We test it all the way you will use it.

    Top-mount Sink Installation in Liberty

    Drop-in sinks are common in Missouri homes with laminate or tile counters. The rim carries the weight. We check the cutout, run a bead of silicone under the lip and tighten the mounting clips evenly around the bowl so the rim seats flat. Next we connect and test the faucet and drain.

    Composite Sink Installation in Liberty

    Granite and quartz composite sinks are tough on top but can chip at the drain opening if a strainer is overtightened. Hand tight comes first. Most composite brands want silicone rather than putty under the strainer because putty can stain them, so we go by the maker's instructions.
