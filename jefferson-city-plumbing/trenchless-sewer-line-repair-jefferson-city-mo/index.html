    Sewer Lining and Pipe Bursting in Jefferson City, MO

    Pipe Bursting in Jefferson City, MO

    Two pits are standard for a burst, one at the house end near the foundation and one out near the street or the city tap. Before pulling, the crew heat-fuses sections of HDPE into one continuous length on the ground. The winch or hydraulic puller then drags the bursting head and the new pipe through. It goes quickly.

    Cured-In-Place Pipe Relining in Jefferson City, MO

    CIPP stands for cured-in-place pipe. We wet out a felt tube with epoxy resin, invert it into the cleaned line with air pressure, and hold it against the old pipe wall until the resin hardens. Cure time runs a few hours. The finished liner seals cracks and leaking joints in the original pipe.

    Causes of Sewer Line Damage in Jefferson City, MO

    Shifting soil is one of the main reasons sewer joints pull apart or crack. Expansive clay soils are common in Missouri, and they swell and shrink with moisture, putting stress on foundations and buried lines. Roots are the other big one, since a hairline gap at a clay joint is enough for a feeder root to get in and then swell into a mat that catches paper. Then age finishes the job. Cast iron scales and thins, and clay bells crack under the weight of soil and traffic.

    Cast Iron Sewer Line Repair in Jefferson City, MO

    Cast iron rusts from the inside out, and the bottom of the pipe goes first. That is called channel rot. We descale the pipe with chain knockers or carbide cutters spinning on a flexible shaft, which brings the wall back to bare metal so a liner can bond to it. Under a slab, lining beats breaking up the floor in two rooms.
