    Common Toilet Repairs in Columbia, MO

    Sensor and Flushometer Repair in Columbia

    Sensor flush valves fail in their own ways, from dead batteries and dirty sensor eyes to solenoids that stick open and keep a bowl running. Before replacing parts, we test the sensor and the solenoid. Batteries are the cheap fix. Ghost flushing on a sensor valve usually means the eye needs adjusting or cleaning.

    Toilet Flush Repair in Columbia, MO

    Two or three tries to get a full flush points to a flapper closing too early, a chain hooked in the wrong hole, scale in the rim jets, or a cracked overflow tube letting water bypass the bowl. You can usually see which one with the lid off. We watch a full cycle with the lid off and replace the flush valve if the overflow tube is cracked, since it cannot be patched.

    Emergency Toilet Repair in Columbia, MO

    An overflowing bowl in a one-bathroom house is an emergency, and so is a burst supply line spraying water across the floor at full house pressure. Shut the angle stop first. Turn the small oval handle behind the toilet clockwise until it stops, then call us so we can walk you through the rest. The first goal on site is stopping the water, then a permanent repair if the part is on the truck.

    Commercial Toilet Repair in Columbia

    Office and retail restrooms take more flushes in a day than a house sees in a month, so the parts wear faster and fail harder. Most commercial toilets use flushometer valves instead of tanks, and those need diaphragm kits and vacuum breakers from time to time. We carry Sloan and Zurn kits. Work can be scheduled before opening or after close.

    Cracked Tank and Supply Line Repair in Columbia, MO

    Braided supply lines last years, but the plastic nut on the toilet end can crack and a bulge in the braid means the inner hose is about to burst. We check every supply line on every visit. Bulges get replaced on the spot. Old vinyl or solid plastic lines get swapped for braided stainless with metal nuts.

    Older Toilet Brands We Service in Columbia, MO

    Older toilets from Eljer, Briggs, Crane and Universal Rundle still turn up in homes built before the 1990s, and their flush valves often take parts nobody stocks anymore. We carry adapters for some of them. For the rest, we will tell you if parts are available or if replacement is the honest answer.

    Fill Valve Replacement in Columbia

    Old ballcock valves with a brass arm and a big plastic float are loud and slow to refill, and they tend to stick open when scale builds up in the ports. We swap them for an adjustable fill valve from Fluidmaster or Korky and set the height so the critical level mark sits at least an inch above the overflow. It is a twenty-minute job. You will notice the difference the first night, because the tank stops hissing.

    After-Hours Toilet Repair in Columbia, MO

    Shutting off the angle stop buys you time, but an old multi-turn stop valve can crumble or leak at the stem when it has not been moved in fifteen years. Do not force it. If it will not close, the main shutoff for the house is the backup, and we can replace the stop with a quarter-turn valve once the water is off.
