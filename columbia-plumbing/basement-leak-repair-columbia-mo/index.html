    Wall and Slab Seepage Repair in Columbia

    Wall Leak Solutions in Columbia, MO

    Damp walls without a crack often point to water soaking through porous block or concrete. A simple foil test tells you whether it is seepage or condensation: tape a square of foil to the wall for a day and see which side gets wet. Condensation needs a dehumidifier.     Groundwater Pressure Relief in Columbia

    High groundwater pushes up through the slab as well as in through the walls. Water rising through floor cracks or around a floor drain during wet weather is the usual sign. A sub-slab drain line to a sump drops the water table right under the floor. It is not a quick job.

    Crack Injection for Basements in Columbia

    Injection works on poured concrete walls, not block. Ports go along the crack, the face gets sealed, and resin goes in from the bottom up until it fills the full depth. Epoxy adds strength. Polyurethane is better for active leaks because it foams on contact with water.

    Supply Line Leak Repair in Columbia

    Water lines along a basement ceiling tend to leak at fittings, valves and pinholes. Homes around Columbia on private wells should test for hardness and bacteria before picking a treatment system. Bad sections get cut out and replaced, and a run with repeated pinholes gets repiped rather than patched. Repiping a basement ceiling is usually a one-day job.

    Sump Pit and Pump Installation in Columbia

    Installing a new sump means breaking out a section of slab, setting a lined pit, and running the discharge out through the rim joist. We use a cast iron pump in most homes and a 1-1/2 inch discharge with a check valve. Discharge cannot go into the sewer in most places. It usually ends outside, well away from the foundation, at a spot your local rules allow.

    Clogged Drain Tile Repair in Columbia

    Footing drains clog with silt and roots over the years, and once they stop flowing the water table at the wall rises. We camera the tile from the sump inlet to see where it is blocked. A jetter usually opens it. If the old clay tile has collapsed, it gets replaced with new perforated pipe.
