    Frequently Asked Questions About Plumbers for Frozen Pipes in St. Charles

    Thawing an accessible frozen pipe in St. Charles usually costs $150 to $400, and burst pipe repair typically runs $300 to $1,000. Prevention costs less. Insulation, heat cable and frost-free faucets usually cost less than one burst repair.

    Thawing and small pipe repairs usually do not need a permit. Bigger work might. Repiping a section, rerouting a line into a heated wall, or replacing a backflow preventer may require a permit in many jurisdictions, and we pull it when needed so the work is on record.

    No pipe is freeze-proof. PEX handles a freeze best. It stretches instead of splitting, but its fittings can still fail, so placement and insulation matter most.

    Insulate exposed pipes in attics, crawlspaces and garages, and seal air leaks around them. Disconnect garden hoses before cold weather. On the coldest nights, let a faucet on an outside wall drip, open the cabinet doors under sinks on exterior walls, and keep the thermostat at the same setting overnight instead of turning it down.

    Most homes have water back within one to two hours of our arrival. Bursts take longer. If the line split, we keep the water off to that branch while we cut out the damaged section and replace it, and in many cases we can isolate that branch so the rest of the house has water during the repair.

    Many policies cover sudden water damage from a burst pipe, though coverage varies. Check your policy terms. We provide photos and a detailed invoice to support your claim, and some policies exclude unheated homes.

    Yes, every repair includes a written workmanship warranty. Your invoice lists the terms. If the repair we made leaks during the warranty period, we come back and fix it, and if a different section of the same exposed run splits in a later freeze, we will quote a permanent fix for the whole run.

    Keep cold air away from the pipe. Insulation and air sealing do most of the work. Close crawlspace vents for the season, insulate attic and garage runs, swap old hose bibs for frost-free models, and add self-regulating heat cable on any run that still gets too cold despite insulation.
