    Hard Water in Missouri: When a Water Softener Pays for Itself

    By Maria Delgado · September 27, 2026 · 5 min read

    What Hard Water Means in Missouri

    Hard water carries dissolved calcium and magnesium picked up from rock and soil, and labs measure it in grains per gallon. Water above roughly 7 grains per gallon counts as hard. Once it passes 10 grains, crust shows up on faucets and spots on glassware within a few weeks. A cheap test strip gives a rough number.

    Groundwater from limestone areas is especially mineral-heavy, which makes softeners and regular water heater flushing worthwhile.

    Your water provider posts a yearly quality report that lists hardness, often in milligrams per liter. To convert it to grains per gallon, divide by 17.1. Wells vary more. A well owner should pay for a full lab test, because iron and manganese in the water change which softener will actually work.

    What Hard Water Costs You

    The water heater takes the worst of it. Scale settles on the bottom of a gas tank and insulates the water from the burner. That popping or rumbling during a heating cycle is the scale, and it means more fuel for the same hot water. Electric elements crust over and burn out early.

    Scale also works on everything else that touches water. Single-handle faucet cartridges get stiff and begin dripping. Dishwasher and washing machine inlet valves clog with grit, and showerheads lose half their spray holes to mineral buildup over a few years. You also use more soap.

    • Water heater: shorter life and higher fuel use
    • Faucets and valves: worn cartridges and drips
    • Tankless units: scale in the heat exchanger and error codes
    • Laundry: stiff towels and more detergent

    When a Softener Pays for Itself

    Run the numbers on your own house. On hard water a tank heater might give you 8 years, and on softened water closer to 12. Add the soap savings and fewer faucet repairs. With four people and water harder than 10 grains, the typical system we put in pays back in about five to seven years.

    Tankless water heaters change the math. Most manufacturers require descaling once a year on hard water, and some void the warranty if scale damage shows up. A softener can stretch that to every few years. Your warranty papers may list a hardness cutoff, usually near 11 or 12 grains.

    Picking the Right Softener

    Size the softener by grains removed per day, not by the box. Multiply your hardness by the gallons your household uses each day, which is about 75 gallons per person. Four people at 12 grains works out to around 3,600 grains a day, which a 32,000 to 40,000 grain unit handles. Oversized units waste salt.

    • Metered regeneration: regenerates based on gallons used and uses less salt
    • Timer regeneration: cheaper, but regenerates on a schedule even when it is not needed
    • Salt-free conditioners: reduce scale buildup but do not remove hardness

    Salt-free conditioners get a lot of advertising. They change the way the minerals behave so less scale sticks to pipes and heaters, but the calcium is still sitting in the water. You will still see spots on glass, and a test strip will read the same as before. Above about 15 grains, only an ion-exchange softener takes the hardness out.

    Getting a Softener Installed

    A softener needs a drain for the brine cycle and a bypass valve, so you can take it offline without shutting off the house. Most installs go near the main line in the garage and take about half a day. We often leave the hose bibs on unsoftened water so you are not spending salt on the lawn. Ask for a hardness test first.
