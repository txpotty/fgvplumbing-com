    Cast Iron Drain Stack Repair in Wildwood, MO

    Old cast iron stacks rot from the inside out. Cracks start along the bottom.     Leaking Drain Pipe Repair in Wildwood, MO

    Drain leaks are low pressure, so they drip instead of spray. They hide well. Corrosion, shifting framing and cracked fittings are the usual causes, and we trace the leak by running each fixture and watching the joints, then cut out the damaged section and replace it with matching PVC, ABS or cast iron. Repairs meet local plumbing code.

    Burst Drain Line Repair in Wildwood, MO

    Drain Vent Repair in Wildwood, MO

    Frequently Asked Questions About Drain Repairs in Wildwood

    Trap and slip-joint repairs typically cost $150 to $350. Pipe sections run higher. A section of drain pipe usually runs $400 to $1,200, and cast iron stack replacement can range from $2,000 to $6,000 with drywall, flooring and access driving most of the spread. You get the price in writing first.

    Small fixture drain repairs usually do not. Stack work typically does. Replacing drain stacks, relocating drains or adding new lines may require one depending on the scope and your local rules. The permit is on us if needed.

    Slow drains, gurgling and sewer smell are the big three warnings. Drips in the cabinet count too. A drain that clogs again a few weeks after it was cleared, or water spots below a bathroom, point to a pipe problem instead of a simple clog. Small leaks get worse.

    Tightening a slip nut or replacing a plastic trap is fair game. Hand snakes are fine too. Anything inside a wall, under a floor or involving cast iron, or a leak that keeps returning after you have tightened it, is a job for a licensed plumber with the right tools. Skip the caustic cleaners.

    Cleaning removes a blockage from a sound pipe. Repair changes the pipe itself. A crack, a sag, a broken fitting or a missing vent will keep causing clogs no matter how often the line gets cleaned, so a drain that clogs repeatedly usually needs repair rather than more snaking. A camera tells you which.

    Age and corrosion lead the list. Settling ground plays a part. Tree roots, temperature swings, heavy loads over buried pipe, and poor original work like unprimed glue joints or missing support straps all cause early failure. It is usually a combination.

    It is high-pressure water cleaning for drain lines. It beats snaking for grease. We use it for severe clogs, scale buildup and fine roots, plus regular cleaning on lines with a history of grease or scale. The pipe gets checked first.

    Repeat clogs come from something in the pipe catching debris. Usually it is buildup. Poor pipe slope, a missing vent or a cracked fitting with a rough edge will snag paper and hair until the line closes off again a few weeks after each cleaning. We find it on video.
