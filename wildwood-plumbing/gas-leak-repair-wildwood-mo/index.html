    What Our Gas Leak Repairs Cover in Wildwood, MO

    Propane Gas Leak Repair in Wildwood, MO

    Propane behaves differently from natural gas because it is heavier than air, so a leak settles into basements and crawlspaces instead of rising. The first-stage regulator at the tank and the second-stage regulator at the house both get checked. Low spots get sniffed first. If the supplier owns the tank, they handle the tank side and we handle the piping from there to the appliances. Rural properties with buried tanks get a line test too.

    Underground Gas Line Repair in Wildwood

    Yard lines run from the meter to the house, and often on to a detached garage, a pool heater, a grill or an outdoor kitchen. Buried steel loses its coating to rocks in the backfill and then rusts through. Roots and settling do damage too. After an 811 locate, we excavate, cut out the failed run and lay polyethylene with a tracer wire beside it. Sand goes around the new pipe before backfill, per Wildwood trench rules.

    Permit and Inspection Coordination in Wildwood

    Replacing more than a connector usually means a permit, and in Wildwood the inspector will want to see a gauge holding pressure before the pipe is covered. We pull the permit and book the inspection ourselves. Most inspections take under twenty minutes. The test is set to whatever the local fuel gas code requires, often a few psi held for fifteen minutes or longer. The signed permit card stays with your house.

    Appliance Gas Line Repair in Wildwood, MO

    Most appliance leaks show up at the flex connector or the shutoff valve behind the appliance, usually right at a flare nut where the two meet. Every time somebody slides the stove out to mop, the connector takes another bend. Valves weep at the stem. The fix is usually a new coated connector and a new ball valve, plus a drip leg if the appliance lacks one. Each joint gets a detector pass and a bubble test before the pilot is lit again.

    Corrugated Stainless Steel Tubing Repair in Wildwood, MO

    CSST is the yellow-jacketed flexible tubing used in many newer homes, and it can be damaged by a nearby lightning strike if it was never bonded. Bonding means a 6 AWG copper wire from the gas piping to the electrical grounding system. Plenty of older installs lack it. We repair CSST only with the matching brand's fittings, because TracPipe and Gastite parts do not interchange. A splice that skips that rule fails inspection.

    Gas Meter to House Line Repair in Wildwood, MO

    The run from the utility meter to the house shutoff is the homeowner's pipe in most service areas, so the repair falls to a plumber and not the gas company. It is also the stretch that takes the most weather, sitting in the sun or the soil. Risers rust at grade. A new riser or a new buried run gets tested and left ready for the utility to turn on. Expect a half day for most riser swap.

    Residential Gas Leak Detection in Wildwood

    A gas leak in a house rarely sits where the smell is strongest, because gas drifts along joists and collects in closets and basements. We start with a gauge on the system to confirm there is a leak at all. Then the sniffer comes out. Our combustible gas detector reads in parts per million, so we can follow the reading fitting by fitting until it peaks. Bubble solution on the suspect joint gives the final answer.

    Gas Line Replacement and Upgrades in Wildwood, MO

    Adding a gas range or a tankless heater can overload pipe that was sized years ago for just a furnace and a water heater. Sizing starts with the total BTU load and the longest run from the meter. Undersized pipe starves burners. Where the old pipe is rusty or poorly threaded, we replace it with black iron or CSST, whichever reaches easier. A big tankless unit may also need a bigger meter from the utility.
