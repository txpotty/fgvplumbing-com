    Well Pump Electrical Troubleshooting in Wildwood, MO

    Electrical faults cause a big share of well calls, from a tripped breaker to a burned contact in the pressure switch. After that, we look at the capacitor and relay in the control box.     Pressure Switch Replacement in Wildwood

    The pressure switch tells the pump when to start and stop. Most switches fail from pitted contacts, a plugged sensing tube or insects in the housing. After swapping the switch and clearing the tube, we set cut-in and cut-out to match the tank, usually 40 and 60 psi. One of the cheaper well repairs.

    Constant Pressure System Repair in Wildwood, MO

    Variable speed drives like the Grundfos SQE and Franklin SubDrive keep pressure steady by changing motor speed. When they fault, the drive shows a code that guides diagnosis. The drive, sensor and wiring get tested, and the failed part gets repaired or replaced before we verify the setpoint. These need a smaller tank.

    Deep Well Pump Repair in Wildwood

    Deep wells get submersible pumps. They push water up instead of pulling it, and they run quiet for years, but motors, impellers and wire splices still fail eventually. We pull the pump with the right equipment, test it and replace only what failed. Heat-shrink splice kits and a torque arrestor protect the new work in your Wildwood, MO well.

    Pressure Tank Problems in Wildwood

    Short cycling is the classic pressure tank symptom, with the pump clicking on and off every few seconds while a faucet runs. A waterlogged tank or a ruptured bladder causes most of it. We check the precharge with the tank drained. It should sit about 2 psi below the cut-in pressure, so a 40/60 switch wants roughly 38 psi in the tank.
