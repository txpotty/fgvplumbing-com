    Water in much of Missouri is moderately hard to hard, so scale gradually builds in water heaters, faucets and appliances. Yes, and mostly in older metal drains. Scale builds up on the inside of tailpieces and traps, narrows the opening, and gives hair and grease a rough surface to grab, which speeds up both clogs and corrosion over the years. Plastic drains resist buildup.

    On a newer drain, just the part. Older chrome or thin brass drains tend to fail joint by joint, so a full replacement is cheaper in the long run. You get both prices.
