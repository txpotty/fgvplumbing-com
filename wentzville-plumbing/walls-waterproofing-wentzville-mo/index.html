    Foundation, Shower and Block Wall Waterproofing in Wentzville, MO

    Interior Walls Waterproofing in Wentzville, MO

    Interior drain tile handles water that already got through the wall. We cut a channel in the slab along the perimeter, lay perforated pipe in washed stone, and tie it into a sump pit so the pump can push the water outside. The wall still gets wet. It just stops puddling on your floor. This route costs less than digging outside and makes sense when a patio or driveway sits against the foundation.

    Shower Wall Waterproofing in Wentzville

    Tile and grout are not waterproof. Water passes through grout every time you shower, so the layer behind the tile, cement board with a membrane or a foam board system, is what actually keeps the studs dry. We tear out failed tile, install new backer, and apply Schluter Kerdi, RedGard or Hydro Ban at the thickness the manufacturer calls for. We reinforce corners, niches and valve penetrations with fabric or preformed pieces.

    Drainage System Integration in Wentzville, MO

    Water has to go somewhere once a wall is sealed. Footing drains, interior drain tile and French drains in the yard all do the same job at different depths. We match the drain to where the water comes from, which might be a high water table, a yard that slopes toward the house, or a gutter dumping at the corner. Downspouts get extended at least four feet out.

    Crawl Space Encapsulation in Wentzville

    Encapsulation seals the whole crawl space, walls and floor, with a reinforced poly liner. We run 12 to 20 mil liner up the walls, tape every seam, seal around piers and pipe penetrations, and usually add a dehumidifier to hold humidity under 60 percent. Water heaters face heavy demand in winter, when incoming water is cold and families use more hot water. The joists stop rotting. Drier air under the floor also cuts down on the musty smell in the house.

    Commercial Walls Waterproofing in Wentzville

    Commercial basements, elevator pits and mechanical rooms in Wentzville take on water the same way houses do, just with more at stake. One wet electrical room can close a business for days. We schedule around operating hours, work with property managers on access, and follow the waterproofing specs written into the building plans for below-grade walls. Downtime stays short.

    Waterproofing for Older Homes in Wentzville, MO

    Houses built before modern drainage rules often have no footing drain at all, or a clay tile drain that collapsed decades ago. Some older neighborhoods still have lead service lines, which are worth replacing when other plumbing work is planned. Fieldstone and old brick foundations need breathable products. A rigid coating on soft brick traps moisture and makes the face spall off over a few seasons, so we use lime-compatible or vapor-open coatings and pair them with interior drain tile. Old mortar joints get repointed first.

    Exterior Foundation Waterproofing in Wentzville

    Exterior waterproofing means digging down to the footing and coating the outside of the wall. It is the most reliable fix for a leaking basement, because the water never touches the block or concrete. We apply a rubberized asphalt or polymer membrane, add a dimple board to protect it, and set perforated drain pipe in gravel along the footing to carry the water away from the wall. Basements are common in many areas, so sump pumps and drain tile are an important part of keeping homes dry. We backfill after the drain is tested.

    Exterior Block Wall Waterproofing in Wentzville, MO

    White crust on block or stem walls is efflorescence, salt left behind as water moves through the masonry and dries. The usual cause is a sprinkler head aimed at the house instead of the bed, or soil graded toward the foundation. We fix the water first. Then we clean the wall, let it dry and apply a breathable masonry sealer or elastomeric coating.

    Our techs have worked in the basements and crawl spaces of Wentzville for years, so they know which house styles tend to leak where. A split-level with a half-buried family room fails differently than a full basement with poured walls. That history speeds up the diagnosis.

    Water in a wall does not wait for a convenient week. We keep open slots for active leaks, and when a pipe is still dripping inside the wall we can shut it off, open the drywall and start drying the cavity that same day. Drying usually takes three to five days.

    Frequently Asked Questions About Walls Waterproofing in Wentzville

    Shower wall waterproofing with a membrane system usually runs $1,200 to $3,500 including demolition and backer board, with the new tile priced separately. Block wall sealing outside often costs $800 to $3,000. Foundation waterproofing below grade is usually $2,500 to $8,000.

    A shower wall job usually takes two to four days from demolition to finished membrane, including the drying time between coats. Exterior block sealing often wraps up in a day. Larger foundation jobs can run several days, and weather can stretch that.

    Check the basement after a heavy rain. Wet spots on the walls, mold or mildew, a musty smell in the crawl space, efflorescence on concrete, new cracks in the foundation or standing water on the floor all mean water is getting in somewhere. Those signs rarely go away on their own.

    Look low on the wall first. Damp patches, a musty smell, mold on the bottom of the drywall, white powdery efflorescence on block or brick, and puddles after rain all point to water coming through. Bowing walls and stair-step cracks need a closer look too.

    Every install gets a written warranty on materials and workmanship. The coverage and the term are spelled out in your proposal, and when a manufacturer offers its own warranty on a membrane or resin we register it in your name. Store the paperwork with your home documents.

    Most systems we install should go 20 to 30 years or longer. Life span comes down to how it was installed, what method was used and what the soil around the house is like. Sump pumps wear out sooner. Plan on a new pump every seven to ten years.

    Anywhere from an afternoon to over a week. Interior crack sealing might take a day or two, while exterior excavation with new footing drains can take several days to more than a week depending on the size of the house. Wet weather can push exterior work back.

    Crack repairs take a few hours. Interior drain tile usually takes one to three days, and a full exterior dig around the whole house can run three to seven days or more depending on the weather and the depth of the footing. We give you a timeline at the first visit.
