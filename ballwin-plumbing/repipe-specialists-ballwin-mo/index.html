    Repiping Work We Handle in Ballwin, MO

    Galvanized Pipe Removal in Ballwin

    Old galvanized lines shed rust flakes that clog aerators, faucet cartridges and toilet fill valves. Clogged aerators are a clue. Replacing galvanized with PEX or copper ends the rust, brings pressure back and stops the leaks that start at the threaded joints where galvanized is thinnest. Old pipe stays in walls when pulling it would wreck finished surfaces.

    Whole-Home Copper Repiping in Ballwin

    Copper is the traditional choice, and Type L copper has a thicker wall than the Type M that went into many older homes. It holds up well, does not mind sunlight in an exposed run and has a long track record behind it. It costs more. We sweat joints with lead-free solder or press them, hang the pipe at proper support intervals and test the whole system before patching.

    Commercial Repiping in Ballwin, MO

    Commercial buildings in Ballwin, MO often have larger pipe, risers that serve several floors and tenants who cannot lose water for a full day. Our crew breaks commercial repipes into zones, working one riser or suite at a time while the rest of the building keeps water. Night work is possible. We use Type L copper or commercial-grade PEX sized from fixture units and installed to commercial code.

    Type L Copper Repiping in Ballwin

    Copper has been plumbing homes for generations, and done right it lasts a long time. We use Type L, not the thinner Type M, and we support it with the right hangers so it does not rub or rattle in the walls. Lead-free solder only. Copper can pinhole in aggressive water, so we ask about your water and any past leaks before recommending it over PEX.

    Kitec and Polybutylene Replacement in Ballwin

    Kitec, an orange or blue PEX-AL-PEX pipe with brass fittings, and gray polybutylene are both known for failures. Both still turn up in older homes. We can usually identify it from the pipe at the water heater, and full replacement makes the house easier to insure and sell.

    Polybutylene Plumbing Replacement in Ballwin, MO

    Polybutylene, a gray or blue plastic pipe installed in many homes from the late 1970s into the mid 1990s, breaks down with exposure to chlorine and other disinfectants in tap water. It fails without warning. The pipe gets brittle from the inside, and fittings crack, so a home that has had one polybutylene leak is usually at risk everywhere else in the system. Full replacement is the fix. Many insurers ask about it.

    Emergency Pipe Failures and Repipes in Ballwin

    Sometimes a house reaches the repipe point all at once, with a burst line and two more leaks right behind it. We stop the active leak first, make temporary repairs that get water back on and then plan the full repipe so you are not rushed into it. That gives you time. Insurance may cover the water damage from the burst even if it does not cover the repipe itself, so we document what we find with photos.

    Copper Repiping in Ballwin, MO

    Some homeowners prefer copper for its long track record and rigidity. Our copper work uses thicker Type L pipe, joined with lead-free solder or ProPress fittings, rather than the Type M found in many older houses. Expect a slightly longer job. Most copper jobs run a day longer than PEX, since each turn needs its own fitting.

    Frequently Asked Questions About Repipe Specialists in Ballwin

    Most whole-home repipes in Ballwin, MO fall somewhere between $4,000 and $15,000. Bigger homes cost more. The final number depends on square footage, how many fixtures need new lines, whether you choose PEX or copper and how much drywall has to be opened and patched along the route. We quote it in writing.

    A whole-home repipe requires permits, pressure testing and code knowledge. Most homeowners are better served by a licensed crew that can finish fast and pass inspection.

    Most repipes in Ballwin, MO take three to five working days for an average house. Larger homes run longer. Copper takes a little longer than PEX because every turn needs a fitting, and the drywall patching usually adds a final day after the plumbing passes inspection.

    A documented repipe removes a known risk that buyers and inspectors look for. Buyers notice it. Replacing polybutylene, Kitec or old galvanized makes a home easier to insure and easier to sell, and a permit record plus a transferable warranty gives the next owner proof the work was done.

    Copper is rigid metal pipe with a long track record. PEX bends instead. PEX is cheaper, quicker and quieter and handles freezes better, while copper tolerates sun and heat and suits owners who want metal, so it depends on your house.

    Homes around Ballwin on private wells should test for hardness and bacteria before picking a filter. Hard water deposits calcium and magnesium scale inside pipe over time. Pressure falls as scale builds. Scale also speeds corrosion in metal lines, which is why PEX-A plus a softener or filter added during a repipe can protect the new system and your fixtures.

    For copper, we use domestic Type L from mills such as Mueller and Cerro. Uponor PEX-A is our usual PEX. We also install Viega and Zurn PEX on some jobs, but we stick with one brand's pipe and fittings throughout a house so the manufacturer warranty stays valid.

    Fixing one leak at a time works until the leaks start coming every few months. That is the turning point. Each repair means a service call, opened drywall and sometimes an insurance claim, while a repipe replaces all of the aging pipe at once and ends the cycle for decades.
