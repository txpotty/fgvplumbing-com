    Frequently Asked Questions About Basement Leak Repair in Ballwin

    Price depends on the source. Fixing a leaking fitting or supply line is usually a few hundred dollars. Crack injection and sump work cost more, and perimeter drains or exterior waterproofing can run several thousand. We quote after testing.

    Tightening a loose fitting or replacing a washer hose is a reasonable DIY task. Cracked drain stacks are not. Hidden slab leaks and sump wiring also belong with a pro, since a mistake can flood the basement or leave an outlet energized in a wet space, and those repairs often need a permit.

    Most supply and drain repairs are finished in a few hours. Sump installs take most of a day. Drain stack replacements and slab leak repairs usually take one to two days, since they involve opening finished surfaces, letting any new concrete patch cure, and running a final test before we close things up.

    Zoeller, Liberty and Wayne cover most of our installs because they are durable and parts are easy to get. Primary pumps get cast iron housings. For homes that lose power in storms, we add a battery backup pump or a water-powered backup where city water pressure is strong enough, and we put a high-water alarm on the pit either way.

    Workmanship on pipe repairs is warranted for the term on your invoice. New pumps carry their own coverage. Seepage repairs carry terms that match the method, and if water returns at the same spot during the warranty period we come back, retest it, and fix it without charging for the labor.

    A pipe or fitting repair usually takes an hour or two. Crack injection takes about half a day. Sumps take most of a day, and perimeter drains take two to five days depending on wall length.

    Plumbing leaks show up no matter the weather. Seepage comes and goes with rain. A moving meter with everything off means a pipe leak, and water that shows up after storms means groundwater.

    Crack repairs use two-part polyurethane or epoxy injection resin, depending on whether the crack is actively wet. Polyurethane handles wet cracks. Interior drains use rigid perforated pipe in washed stone and a dimpled plastic membrane on the wall, which is more reliable than the flexible corrugated pipe some installers use because it holds its slope and can be jetted.
