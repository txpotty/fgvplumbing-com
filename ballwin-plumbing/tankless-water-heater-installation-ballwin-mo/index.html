    Tank to Tankless Conversions in Ballwin

    Conversions in Ballwin usually take a full day. The old tank gets drained and hauled off, the gas line is upsized and tested, a new vent goes out through a wall or roof, and the unit is mounted, plumbed with isolation valves and fired. You shower that night. The inspector comes after.

    Recirculation Systems for Tankless Heaters in Ballwin

    Tankless heaters can take a few extra seconds to get hot water to a far bathroom, because the unit has to sense flow and fire first. A recirc pump on a timer or sensor keeps the hot line primed, cutting the wait at the far sink from about a minute to a few seconds. It saves water too.

    Gas Tankless Water Heater Installation in Ballwin

    Gas tankless units in Ballwin are the common pick for whole-house hot water because a single 199,000 BTU burner can feed two showers and a sink at the same time without losing temperature. The catch is gas supply. In most houses, the line from the meter has to be upsized, often from half-inch to three-quarter inch or bigger. We test the new gas piping before first fire.

    Tankless Water Heater Replacement in Ballwin, MO

    Replacing an old tankless unit is usually simpler than a first-time conversion. Since the gas line and vent are already there, most swaps mean matching connections, inspecting the old vent and mounting the new heater. Vent sizes can change by brand. We check before ordering.

    Hard Water Protection for Tankless Units in Ballwin, MO

    Calcium scale is the most common reason tankless heaters fail. Water in much of Missouri is moderately hard to hard, so scale gradually builds in water heaters, faucets and fixtures. Scale coats the heat exchanger, cuts efficiency and triggers error codes, and left alone it can crack the exchanger and end the warranty claim before it starts. Flush it yearly. We install isolation valves for that.

    Electric Tankless Water Heater Installation in Ballwin, MO

    Electric tankless heaters make sense in Ballwin, MO homes without gas, or for a single sink or a guest bath far from the main heater. A whole-home electric unit draws heavy amperage. Some need two or three 40 to 60 amp double-pole breakers, and a 100-amp panel often cannot carry that on top of the air conditioner and range. We check the panel first.
