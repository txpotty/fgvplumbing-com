    PEX-A Whole-Home Repiping in Lee's Summit

    PEX-A is flexible, resists scale and handles thermal expansion well, which suits hard water and long attic runs. We use Uponor or similar PEX-A with cold expansion fittings that open the pipe end and let it shrink back over the fitting for a full-bore joint. Fewer joints, fewer leaks. Most homes are repiped with PEX-A in two to three days.

    Commercial Repipe Services in Lee's Summit

    Older commercial buildings in Lee's Summit often have galvanized risers, copper that has pinholed or plastic pipe that no longer meets code. We replace supply risers, branch lines and drains while keeping offices, shops or apartments running as much as possible. Tenants get notice of every shutoff. Work is phased by zone, and we coordinate access, keys and noise limits with the property manager.

    Copper Repiping in Lee's Summit, MO

    Some homeowners prefer copper for its long track record and rigidity. We install Type L copper, which has a thicker wall than the Type M used in many older homes, with lead-free solder or ProPress fittings. Expect a slightly longer job. Most copper jobs run a day longer than PEX, since each turn needs its own fitting.

    Polybutylene Plumbing Replacement in Lee's Summit, MO

    Polybutylene, a gray or blue plastic pipe installed in many homes from the late 1970s into the mid 1990s, breaks down with exposure to chlorine and other disinfectants in tap water. It fails without warning. Brittle pipe walls and cracked fittings mean one polybutylene leak usually signals risk across the whole house. Full replacement is the fix. Insurers often ask about it at renewal.

    PEX Versus Copper Repipe Pricing in Lee's Summit, MO

    PEX repipes usually cost less than copper because the material is cheaper and installation goes faster. Copper costs more. Copper commonly costs 20 to 40 percent more than PEX, and some owners prefer it anyway for its long history. Our quotes for either include permit, fittings, patching and cleanup.

    Slab Leak Reroutes in Lee's Summit

    When one line under the slab leaks, a full repipe is not always the first step. Rerouting one branch overhead and leaving the bad pipe in the slab is often cheaper than both a repipe and a jackhammer repair. It suits a first-time leak.
