    Water Line Repairs We Handle in Independence, MO

    Galvanized Water Line Replacement in Independence, MO

    Galvanized service lines were common in houses built before the 1970s, and a lot of them are still in the ground. Groundwater from limestone areas is especially hard, which makes softeners and regular water heater flushing worthwhile. Rust and scale close the pipe from the inside. Rusty water at the first tap in the morning is a classic sign, and so is pressure that falls off when the shower and the washer run together. Replacing the full run with copper or PEX clears all of it up. Most homes are done in a day.

    PEX and Copper Water Line Repair in Independence

    Copper and PEX fail in different places. Copper tends to pinhole where a rock rubs it or where the water chemistry eats the wall from inside. With PEX, the fitting is the weak point most of the time. We cut out the bad section of copper and sweat or press in a new length, and for PEX we use the matching crimp or expansion tool so the joint is made the way the manufacturer intends.

    Pressure Regulator and Shutoff Repair in Independence

    High static pressure shortens the life of the service line and every fixture behind it. Most codes cap it at 80 psi. We gauge the pressure at a hose bib, then swap a failed regulator for a new Watts or Zurn Wilkins valve when it reads too high. Old gate valves that will not close get replaced with full-port ball valves. That way you can actually shut the water off.

    Main Water Line Repair in Independence

    Your main line is the pipe from the meter to the house, usually 3/4 inch or 1 inch, buried anywhere from a foot to several feet down depending on frost depth. When it fails you tend to see wet ground, a spinning meter and weak pressure at every tap. We start by closing the house shutoff and watching the dial. A moving dial with the house valve closed means an underground leak. Then a ground microphone narrows the spot, and we dig one hole instead of a trench across your yard.

    Commercial Water Line Services in Independence, MO

    Besides repairs, FGV Plumbing handles service line upgrades when a commercial tenant needs more flow, for example when a restaurant adds fixtures or a gym adds showers. Bigger meters need bigger pipe. We size it to the fixture load. Then we coordinate with the water provider on the tap and meter change, set the backflow assembly and get the inspection signed off, so the space can open on schedule.

    Trenchless Water Line Replacement in Independence

    A trench through a paver driveway or a mature garden can cost more to restore than the pipe itself. That is where trenchless pays off. We dig a pit at each end and pull HDPE or PEX through with a boring tool or pipe puller, following the old line or cutting a new path beside it. Most replacements finish in a day. You also get the manufacturer warranty on the new pipe.

    Underground Water Line Leak Repair in Independence

    Underground leaks hide well. Many people find out from the bill before they see anything in the yard. Others notice a patch of grass that grows greener than the rest, or a spot near the walk that stays muddy days after rain. We confirm the leak at the meter, trace the pipe route and listen along it. The dig follows the sound.

    Slab Leak Water Line Repair in Independence

    A slab leak is a supply pipe leaking under the concrete floor of the house. Older brick homes in Missouri often still have galvanized supply pipe and cast iron drains, which corrode and narrow over time. Signs include a warm spot on the floor, the sound of running water and a meter that moves with everything off. We pinpoint the leak with acoustic gear and thermal imaging. From there you have three choices: break the slab at the spot, reroute the line through the walls or attic, or abandon the old pipe under the floor and run new PEX above it. You get a price on every option.

    Frequently Asked Questions About Water Line Repair in Independence

    Spot repairs on an accessible line usually run $350 to $1,500. The deeper the pipe, the higher the price. A trenched meter-to-house replacement is typically $1,800 to $5,500, while trenchless replacement tends to run $3,000 to $7,000 since it needs a boring rig or puller. The written price comes before we start.

    Spot repairs usually take a few hours. Plan on a day for a full replacement. Jobs with a deep line, a lot of hardscape or a trenchless pull sometimes run two or three days when concrete has to be cut and patched or the inspector cannot come right away. We give you a time estimate with the quote.

    We work on copper, PEX, HDPE poly, PVC and old galvanized steel. Polybutylene too, when we find it. For new work in Independence, Type K copper and burial-rated PEX are our usual picks, with HDPE for pulled lines. The trucks carry transition fittings for every one of them.

    Look for a wet or extra-green patch in the yard. Listen at the meter box for hissing. Low pressure at every fixture, rusty water and a climbing bill are other common clues. The surest check is your meter. If it moves with everything off, you have a leak.

    A typical spot repair runs two to four hours, including the locate and backfill. Replacements take longer. Deep lines, long runs and jobs under concrete or pavers add time for cutting the slab, restoring the surface and waiting on the inspector to show up. You get a realistic timeline when we hand you the quote.

    Watch the bill first. A sudden jump with no change in use is the classic clue. Other signs include low pressure everywhere, soggy ground in dry weather, discolored water and a hissing sound near the meter. On a slab home, a warm spot on the floor can mean the leak is under the house.

    In most places the water provider owns the meter and the pipe on the street side of it. You own the rest. Some providers draw the line at the curb stop instead, so before quoting we check exactly where the leak is. If it turns out to be theirs, we tell you and help you report it.

    For PEX we use Uponor and Viega, depending on the job. Buried copper is always Type K. Couplings and transition fittings come from makers like Mueller and Ford, and we stick with fittings rated for direct burial in the soil conditions we find. We do not use cheap knockoff fittings.
