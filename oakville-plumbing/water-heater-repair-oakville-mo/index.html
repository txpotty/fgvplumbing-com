    Anode Rod Replacement in Oakville, MO

    The anode rod sacrifices itself so the steel tank does not rust. In hard water it can be eaten away within a few years. We pull and inspect the rod, then install a magnesium, aluminum or powered anode depending on your water and any odor problems. Rotten-egg odor often clears up.

    Gas Burner and Venting Repair in Oakville, MO

    Combustion is the part of a gas heater that matters most for safety. Yellow flame tips or soot at the draft hood mean the burner is not getting enough air to burn cleanly. We clean the burner and check draft with a mirror or a smoke test. Backdrafting is dangerous.

    No Hot Water Diagnostics in Oakville

    Diagnosis starts with fuel. We confirm the gas is on, the breaker is set and the pilot is lit. From there we test the thermostat, element or gas valve one at a time and recommend the cheapest repair that will actually hold up. Simple checks first.

    Pilot Repair in Oakville

    A pilot that lights and then dies when you let go of the knob almost always means a weak thermocouple. Less often it is a dirty flame arrestor screen choking off combustion air. We test the thermocouple output in millivolts. A good reading points us to the gas valve instead.

    Electric Water Heater Element Repair in Oakville

    Lukewarm water or hot water that runs out in five minutes usually means a burned-out element or a stuck thermostat. We check each element with an ohmmeter. A shorted element will trip the breaker, while an open one just sits there cold, and the readings tell us which one is which. Parts for most tanks ride on the truck.

    Leaking Water Heater Repair in Oakville, MO

    Not every leak means a dead tank. Relief valve, drain valve and supply line drips are cheap fixes. Water from the tank body or bottom seam usually means the lining has failed, and replacement is the only real fix. We protect your floors either way.

    Thermocouples, flame sensors, igniters and gas control valves cause most gas heater failures. Each part gets tested, the burner gets cleaned and the vent gets a draft check. A pilot that lights and then goes out a few seconds later usually points to a weak thermocouple or a clogged flame arrestor screen. Most are done in under two hours.
